# Syntropix Assess — Scenario Authoring Standard v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: PRE-ITEM BUILD STANDARD
Applies to: Manager Signal, Campus Signal

## Purpose

Every scored scenario/testlet must be written from a controlled construct and scoring architecture. This standard prevents attractive but psychometrically weak scenarios, hidden domain-knowledge traps, obvious socially desirable answers, and uncontrolled item-writing drift.

## 1. Scenario anatomy

Each scenario record must contain:

- product: Manager Signal / Campus Signal
- construct
- subconstruct
- job family lens
- role level
- CX complexity level
- intended empirical difficulty band
- scenario stem
- evidence/data blocks
- decision prompt
- response options or constructed response specification
- scoring rubric
- rationale for each score point
- critical constraint(s)
- acceptable trade-offs
- unacceptable risk(s)
- challenge-back variant
- AI rule
- accessibility notes
- SME review status
- item version

## 2. Construct purity

A scenario must have one primary scored construct.

Secondary tensions may be present, but the item cannot require success on an unrelated hidden capability.

Example:
A People Judgment item may contain delivery pressure, but it must not become primarily a finance-knowledge problem.

## 3. Realism standard

Scenarios should resemble recognizable work decisions:
- imperfect information;
- plausible stakeholders;
- realistic constraints;
- credible consequences.

Avoid:
- cartoon villains;
- obviously unethical distractors;
- impossible timelines;
- contrived “best practice” wording;
- trivia disguised as judgment.

## 4. Response-option quality

For selected-response items:
- all options should be plausible;
- at least two should have defensible advantages;
- weak options should fail for a specific job-relevant reason;
- avoid one “HR textbook” answer against three absurd choices;
- avoid moral language that reveals the key;
- avoid option length/style clues.

## 5. Stateful design

Where used, a testlet should progress through:
1. initial situation;
2. candidate decision;
3. consequence;
4. new evidence / changed constraint;
5. revised decision;
6. optional challenge-back.

Later stages may depend on earlier choices, but branching must preserve comparability through planned evidence coverage.

## 6. Complexity construction

CX1 Clear Context:
- low ambiguity;
- one main tension;
- visible consequences.

CX2 Competing Priorities:
- two legitimate demands;
- moderate trade-off.

CX3 Ambiguous Trade-offs:
- incomplete/conflicting evidence;
- several plausible paths;
- multiple stakeholders.

CX4 High-Consequence Complexity:
- significant consequence;
- interdependent stakeholders;
- no clean answer;
- high cost of poor sequencing/judgment.

Complexity must come from the work situation—not obscure vocabulary or hidden knowledge.

## 7. Difficulty hypothesis

Authors may propose an intended empirical difficulty band, but must label it as a hypothesis.

No item is officially “hard” until response data supports that conclusion.

## 8. Manager Signal authoring rules

Prefer:
- managerial consequences;
- people + execution tensions;
- evidence updating;
- stakeholder pressure;
- role-authority boundaries.

Avoid:
- personality self-report;
- “what would a good manager do?” obviousness;
- requiring company-specific policy knowledge unless supplied in the scenario.

## 9. Campus Signal authoring rules

Prefer:
- novel information supplied inside assessment;
- workplace situations understandable without extensive job history;
- learning transfer;
- evidence interpretation;
- early-career communication/escalation judgment.

Avoid:
- prestige-coded language;
- hidden corporate etiquette;
- advanced business knowledge;
- socioeconomic familiarity as an advantage.

## 10. AI-era item rules

AI scenarios must assess:
- task suitability;
- verification;
- privacy/confidentiality;
- unsupported output;
- accountable human ownership;
- escalation where relevant.

Do not assess:
- product-brand trivia;
- prompt fashion;
- enthusiasm for AI.

## 11. Fairness review prompts

For every scenario ask:
- Does success require cultural familiarity not essential to the job?
- Does wording advantage a particular socioeconomic/educational background?
- Is there unnecessary reading complexity?
- Could disability/accommodation affect performance unrelated to construct?
- Is gender/race/age or another protected characteristic encoded into the story unnecessarily?
- Does the item reward deference to hierarchy where constructive challenge may be job-relevant?

## 12. Accessibility standard

- plain language;
- avoid unnecessarily long stems;
- evidence blocks visually separable;
- tables/charts accessible;
- no colour-only meaning;
- keyboard compatible;
- timing burden reviewed.

## 13. SME review

Minimum pre-pilot:
- one role/context SME;
- one assessment/method reviewer;
- one fairness/accessibility review.

High-risk CX4 or family-specific scenarios may require additional SMEs.

## 14. Item rejection criteria

Reject/rewrite when:
- SMEs cannot agree what high-quality judgment looks like;
- construct is unclear;
- answer key depends on unstated assumptions;
- one option is obviously socially desirable;
- domain knowledge overwhelms intended construct;
- scenario cannot be made accessible without changing meaning;
- scoring rationale cannot be written transparently.

## 15. Authoring sequence

1. construct/subconstruct selected;
2. job context selected;
3. critical tension defined;
4. CX level defined;
5. ideal evidence dimensions written;
6. scenario stem drafted;
7. response paths drafted;
8. scoring rubric written;
9. challenge-back designed;
10. fairness/accessibility review;
11. SME review;
12. cognitive test;
13. pilot;
14. empirical calibration.

No production release before this chain is satisfied.
