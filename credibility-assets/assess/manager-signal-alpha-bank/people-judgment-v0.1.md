## PJ-01 — High performer, deteriorating team climate
Construct: People Judgment
Subconstructs: accountability; team impact; proportional response
Lens: universal manager core
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
A strong performer consistently delivers above target. Two team members tell you that the person dismisses others' ideas and interrupts them in meetings. You have not personally observed a serious incident.

What would you do first?

A. Tell the employee immediately that the behaviour must stop and place the issue on record. **1**
B. Speak separately with the people raising the concern, gather specific examples, then discuss the pattern and expectations with the employee. **3**
C. Leave it alone unless delivery begins to fall; high performers often have direct communication styles. **0**
D. Address the whole team at the next meeting about respectful communication without naming anyone. **2**

Rationale:
B balances fact-finding, individual accountability and team protection. D may help norms but avoids the individual issue. A acts before clarifying facts. C discounts team impact.

### Stage 2 — New evidence
You learn that similar feedback was given informally to the employee six months ago. The employee says colleagues are “too sensitive” and points to their results.

A. Set clear behavioural expectations, explain the team impact, agree observable changes and follow up on both performance and conduct. **3**
B. Move the employee to work independently so the team is less exposed. **1**
C. Focus on results and ask colleagues to adapt because losing the employee would hurt delivery. **0**
D. Ask HR to handle the matter from this point because it has become sensitive. **2**

### Stage 3 — Challenge-back
A critical customer now asks specifically for this employee on a high-risk project. Your director says, “Don't destabilise the account this quarter.”

A. Pause any behavioural intervention until the customer project ends. **0**
B. Keep the employee on the work but continue the behaviour plan, increase monitoring and protect affected colleagues through clear operating boundaries. **3**
C. Remove the employee from the account immediately regardless of delivery consequences. **1**
D. Ask the team whether they are willing to tolerate the behaviour temporarily. **1**

High-quality evidence:
maintains accountability under commercial pressure; avoids both avoidance and disproportionate action; separates business continuity from conduct standards.

## PJ-02 — New manager inherits an underperformer
Construct: People Judgment
Subconstructs: diagnosis; coaching; accountability
Initial CX: CX1
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
You inherit a team member whose output has been below target for two months. The previous manager left little documentation.

What would you do first?

A. Start a formal performance process immediately because the target has already been missed for two months. **1**
B. Review available performance evidence, clarify expectations with the employee and understand barriers before deciding the intervention. **3**
C. Give the employee another month without discussing performance so you can form your own view. **1**
D. Ask peers whether the employee has a poor attitude. **0**

### Stage 2 — New evidence
The employee recently took responsibility for a new process and has never been trained on it. Their performance on older responsibilities remains acceptable.

A. Reset expectations, provide targeted support/training and define a short review period with measurable outcomes. **3**
B. Reduce targets permanently because the role has changed. **1**
C. Remove the new responsibility and return the employee to their old scope. **2**
D. Continue with formal performance action because training should not be necessary at this level. **0**

### Stage 3 — New consequence
After training, performance improves on the new process but another recurring task begins slipping.

A. Conclude that the employee lacks capability and escalate immediately. **1**
B. Review workload/capacity and prioritisation with the employee, then establish explicit commitments across the full role. **3**
C. Keep adding training until every task reaches target. **1**
D. Quietly redistribute the slipping task to a stronger team member. **0**

## PJ-03 — Confidential concern involving a peer manager
Construct: People Judgment
Subconstructs: confidentiality; employee safety; escalation judgment
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
An employee from another team asks for a private conversation. They describe repeated humiliating comments from their manager but ask you not to tell anyone because they fear retaliation.

A. Promise complete confidentiality before hearing further details. **1**
B. Explain that you will treat the information carefully but may need to escalate depending on what you learn, then listen and document the concern accurately. **3**
C. Tell the employee to raise it directly with their manager first. **0**
D. Immediately contact the manager to get their side of the story. **0**

### Stage 2
The employee shares specific examples and says two colleagues witnessed them.

A. Keep the matter confidential because the employee asked you to. **0**
B. Explain the next appropriate escalation route, protect disclosure to the minimum necessary group and avoid investigating beyond your mandate. **3**
C. Ask the witnesses yourself before telling HR/People. **1**
D. Send the allegations to the leadership team to ensure transparency. **0**

### Stage 3
You learn the accused manager owns a business-critical launch and senior leadership is worried about disruption.

A. Delay escalation until after the launch. **0**
B. Continue the governed escalation and separately plan business continuity so operational pressure does not determine whether the concern is handled. **3**
C. Remove the manager immediately before any formal review. **1**
D. Ask the employee whether the concern can wait two weeks. **0**
