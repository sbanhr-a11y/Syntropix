## DE-01 — Two dashboards disagree
Construct: Decision & Evidence Quality
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
Two dashboards report materially different customer-retention numbers. Leadership wants a recommendation today.

A. Use the dashboard your team normally trusts. **1**
B. Identify the definition/data-source difference that could explain the disagreement before basing the recommendation on either number. **3**
C. Average the two results. **0**
D. Choose the more conservative figure. **1**

### Stage 2
You learn one dashboard updates daily and the other monthly, but the monthly metric has historically been the official measure.

A. Use the official monthly metric only. **2**
B. Use both deliberately: official measure for reported performance, daily signal for emerging risk, and explain the distinction. **3**
C. Replace the official measure with the daily dashboard. **1**
D. Delay all decisions until the month closes. **1**

### Stage 3
The daily signal suggests a sudden deterioration not yet visible in the official metric.

A. Ignore it until the official number confirms it. **0**
B. Take proportionate investigative/action steps based on the leading signal while clearly labelling uncertainty. **3**
C. Announce that retention has fallen. **1**
D. Immediately change the team's quarterly target. **1**

## DE-02 — Successful pilot, weak comparison
Construct: Decision & Evidence Quality
Subconstruct: causal caution; reversibility
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
A new process was piloted in one team. Productivity rose 12% during the pilot. The sponsor wants immediate company-wide rollout.

A. Roll it out; 12% is strong enough evidence. **1**
B. Examine baseline differences, concurrent changes and operational risks before treating the improvement as caused by the process. **3**
C. Reject the process because one team is never enough. **1**
D. Ask whether employees liked it and use that as the deciding factor. **0**

### Stage 2
The pilot team also received additional staffing during the same period.

A. Conclude the process did not work. **1**
B. Treat attribution as uncertain and design a staged rollout/comparison that can separate effects better. **3**
C. Ignore staffing because productivity is the business outcome that matters. **0**
D. Roll out only to high-performing teams. **1**

### Stage 3
The sponsor says delaying rollout could lose a quarter of benefit.

A. Run a reversible limited expansion with predefined success/failure measures rather than choose between full rollout and no action. **3**
B. Roll out fully because opportunity cost is now high. **1**
C. Stop the project until a perfect experiment is possible. **1**
D. Let each manager decide locally. **0**

## DE-03 — Vendor recommendation under uncertainty
Construct: Decision & Evidence Quality
Subconstruct: source quality; risk
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
A vendor provides a polished analysis showing its solution will save 20% annually. The assumptions are not fully disclosed.

A. Use the figure because the vendor has specialist expertise. **0**
B. Request assumptions/source data and independently test the inputs material to the decision. **3**
C. Discount the saving to 10% and proceed. **1**
D. Reject the vendor for lack of transparency. **2**

### Stage 2
The vendor discloses assumptions, but one major assumption depends on unusually high adoption.

A. Build scenarios using realistic adoption ranges and assess whether the decision remains attractive under lower uptake. **3**
B. Use the vendor's case because they know implementation best. **0**
C. Replace the assumption with the lowest imaginable adoption. **1**
D. Ask the vendor to guarantee the savings contractually and ignore the model. **1**

### Stage 3
Your executive sponsor prefers this vendor and asks for approval before quarter-end.

A. Approve and document that the sponsor accepted the risk. **1**
B. Present the decision range, key uncertainty and mitigation, then seek approval against explicit assumptions rather than presenting one point estimate. **3**
C. Delay indefinitely. **1**
D. Choose another vendor to avoid perceived bias. **0**
