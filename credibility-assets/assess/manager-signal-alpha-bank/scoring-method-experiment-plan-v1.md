# Manager Signal — Scoring Method Experiment Plan v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: PILOT DESIGN / PRE-EMPIRICAL

## Why this experiment is required

SJT research does not establish one universally superior scoring method. Scoring keys can be built from:
- best/worst expert judgments;
- rank ordering;
- effectiveness ratings;
- consensus distance;
- other empirically derived approaches.

Syntropix will therefore collect richer SME information than the final candidate score initially requires.

## Candidate response format — current hypothesis

For speed and candidate clarity, the primary Manager Signal format remains:

> **What would you do? Choose the response that most closely reflects your likely action.**

Candidate selects one option.

This behavioural-tendency instruction is controlled because SJT response instructions can materially change the construct being measured.

## SME key data to collect

For every response option, each SME independently provides:

1. effectiveness rating: 1–7;
2. rank within the option set;
3. unacceptable-risk flag: yes/no;
4. critical-constraint protection: yes/partly/no;
5. rationale.

After independent rating, SMEs may discuss substantive disagreement, but original independent ratings are retained.

## Provisional scoring variants to compare

### Model A — Ordinal Expert Key
Current 0–3 category key.

Advantages:
- highly interpretable;
- simple reporting;
- supports partial credit.

Risk:
- category boundaries may lose information.

### Model B — Normalised SME Effectiveness
Candidate receives the normalized mean SME effectiveness of the selected option.

Advantages:
- preserves more expert-rating information.

Risk:
- apparent numerical precision may exceed key reliability.

### Model C — Rank-Based Key
Selected option receives credit based on expert rank position.

Advantages:
- less dependent on absolute rating-scale usage.

Risk:
- treats gaps between ranks as equivalent.

### Model D — Strict Best-Option Key
Credit only for selecting the highest-rated option.

Advantages:
- simple.

Risk:
- discards useful partial-quality information and may reduce nuance.

## Pilot comparison criteria

Compare scoring models on:
- score distribution;
- reliability/precision appropriate to test structure;
- item/testlet discrimination;
- criterion relationships where available;
- subgroup differences/fairness;
- sensitivity to SME disagreement;
- interpretability;
- incremental value;
- stability under resampling/cross-validation where feasible.

No scoring model wins solely because it produces the largest variance or highest coefficient.

## Stateful scoring

Separately compare whether adding adaptive-update evidence improves prediction/interpretation beyond Stage 1 decisions.

Candidate should not automatically gain points for changing a decision. Update quality depends on whether new evidence justified the change.

## Complexity

Do not automatically give more points for CX4.

Test whether:
- higher-CX responses add incremental information;
- complexity-aware weighting improves criterion evidence;
- weighting increases subgroup differences or noise.

Until supported, Complexity Range remains descriptive.

## SME-key quality

For each item report:
- mean effectiveness by option;
- dispersion;
- rank agreement;
- unacceptable-risk agreement;
- qualitative disagreement.

Items with unresolved expert disagreement should be revised/retired rather than “averaged into certainty.”

## Decision rule

The final operational scoring key will be selected after pilot evidence and documented in the technical manual with:
- rationale;
- population;
- evidence;
- fairness analysis;
- limitations;
- version.

The existence of an expert key does not by itself validate the assessment.
