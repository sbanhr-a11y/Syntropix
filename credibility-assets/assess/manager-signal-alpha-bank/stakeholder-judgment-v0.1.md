## SJ-01 — Senior leader asks to bypass process
Construct: Stakeholder Judgment
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
A senior leader asks you to bypass a standard approval because “this is urgent and everyone agrees.”

A. Comply because the request comes from an accountable senior leader. **0**
B. Clarify the reason for the control, determine whether an authorised exception route exists, and explain the fastest compliant path. **3**
C. Refuse without discussion. **1**
D. Ask a colleague what they usually do. **1**

### Stage 2
The control exists because similar past exceptions created customer risk.

A. Escalate the urgency and risk through the authorised route rather than bypassing the control. **3**
B. Make the exception once and document it. **0**
C. Tell the senior leader the rule is non-negotiable and end the discussion. **2**
D. Delay responding until the urgency passes. **0**

### Stage 3
The leader says the delay could cost a major client and asks you to “own the commercial consequence.”

A. Bypass the control to protect the client. **0**
B. Make the trade-off explicit and escalate to the decision authority empowered to accept both commercial and control risk. **3**
C. Refuse and tell the leader the loss is their responsibility. **1**
D. Ask the client for more time without internal escalation. **1**

## SJ-02 — Customer commitment beyond current capability
Construct: Stakeholder Judgment
Subconstruct: expectation management; boundary
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
A major customer asks for a feature by next month. Sales has implied it is likely, but product has not committed.

A. Tell the customer it should be possible to preserve confidence. **0**
B. Clarify the business need, avoid confirming an unapproved date, and align internally on options before making a commitment. **3**
C. Tell the customer Sales made a mistake. **1**
D. Say no immediately because product has not approved it. **1**

### Stage 2
Product says the feature could be delivered only by delaying another committed customer feature.

A. Commit because this customer is larger. **1**
B. Surface the cross-customer trade-off and seek the accountable prioritisation decision with clear impact. **3**
C. Ask engineering to work faster. **0**
D. Split the difference and promise a partial feature without validation. **1**

### Stage 3
Sales leadership pressures you to give the customer a date before internal prioritisation is resolved.

A. Give a provisional date and explain it might move. **1**
B. Provide a decision date/process rather than an unapproved delivery promise. **3**
C. Stop engaging with the customer until leadership resolves it. **0**
D. Tell the customer Sales is pressuring you. **0**

## SJ-03 — Cross-functional ownership gap
Construct: Stakeholder Judgment
Subconstruct: influence; accountability
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
A recurring customer issue spans your team and another function. Each team believes the other owns the root cause.

A. Escalate both teams to senior leadership immediately. **1**
B. Bring the relevant owners together around the customer impact, map responsibilities/dependencies and establish one accountable resolution path. **3**
C. Fix the issue within your team even if the cause is elsewhere. **2**
D. Document that the other function owns it. **0**

### Stage 2
The other manager agrees to actions but repeatedly misses them.

A. Keep reminding them informally. **1**
B. Make commitments, dates and impact visible; address the missed commitments directly and escalate only if the dependency remains unresolved. **3**
C. Escalate them as unreliable. **1**
D. Absorb the work permanently into your team. **0**

### Stage 3
Senior leadership asks which team caused the problem.

A. Defend your team and provide evidence that the other function missed actions. **1**
B. Explain the shared failure mode, distinguish accountabilities factually and focus on the control/process needed to prevent recurrence. **3**
C. Avoid answering to preserve the relationship. **0**
D. Say both teams were equally responsible even if evidence differs. **1**
