## LA-01 — Strategy no longer producing expected result
Construct: Learning & Adaptive Judgment
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
A strategy you introduced worked well for two quarters. Results have now declined for six weeks.

A. Stay the course longer because the strategy has already proven itself. **1**
B. Examine whether conditions, execution or assumptions changed before deciding whether to adjust the strategy. **3**
C. Replace the strategy immediately. **1**
D. Increase pressure on the team to execute it more consistently. **0**

### Stage 2
Data shows execution quality remained stable, but customer behaviour changed.

A. Adapt the strategy to the changed behaviour and define indicators for whether the revision works. **3**
B. Increase incentives so customers behave as before. **1**
C. Continue because six weeks may still be temporary. **1**
D. Blame external conditions and lower expectations. **0**

### Stage 3
Your original strategy was publicly praised by senior leadership and is associated with your reputation.

A. Maintain it until there is overwhelming evidence. **0**
B. Recommend revision openly, explain what changed and preserve only the components still supported by evidence. **3**
C. Quietly modify it without calling attention to the change. **2**
D. Ask a peer to propose the change so it is not seen as reversing your own decision. **0**

## LA-02 — Feedback challenges manager's self-view
Construct: Learning & Adaptive Judgment
Subconstruct: feedback use; experimentation
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
Your engagement survey shows team members want more autonomy. You believe you already delegate extensively.

A. Explain to the team that they may not recognise how much autonomy they already have. **0**
B. Seek specific examples of where people experience unnecessary control and compare them with your intended delegation. **3**
C. Delegate more tasks immediately. **2**
D. Ignore one survey result until next cycle. **1**

### Stage 2
Several examples show you frequently revise employees' work before stakeholders see it.

A. Stop reviewing work completely. **1**
B. Distinguish high-risk outputs from normal work and test lighter review/checkpoints where risk permits. **3**
C. Continue because final quality remains your accountability. **1**
D. Ask employees to improve quality so you do not need to review. **1**

### Stage 3
One lightly reviewed output contains a visible mistake.

A. Return to reviewing everything. **0**
B. Diagnose whether the mistake reflects the delegation model, capability or a missing control; adjust proportionately rather than abandon the experiment. **3**
C. Publicly correct the employee so standards are clear. **0**
D. Quietly fix it and continue unchanged. **1**

## LA-03 — New operating model changes responsibilities
Construct: Learning & Adaptive Judgment
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
A new operating model transfers part of your team's decision authority to a central function. Your team believes the change will slow work.

A. Resist the model until leadership proves it will improve outcomes. **1**
B. Clarify the intended decision boundaries, identify likely friction points and test how the new model works in real cases. **3**
C. Tell the team to comply without debate. **1**
D. Continue making decisions as before until someone objects. **0**

### Stage 2
Early cases show the central function is slower, but it also catches risks your team previously missed.

A. Argue to reverse the model because speed is worse. **1**
B. Use the evidence to propose clearer thresholds: central review for higher-risk cases, faster local handling for lower-risk ones if governance allows. **3**
C. Accept the slower process entirely because risk control matters more. **2**
D. Bypass the central team for urgent cases informally. **0**

### Stage 3
The central leader initially rejects your threshold proposal.

A. Escalate them as obstructive. **0**
B. Bring specific case evidence, acknowledge the risk-control benefit and refine the proposal around shared outcomes. **3**
C. Stop proposing changes. **1**
D. Tell your team to use workarounds. **0**
