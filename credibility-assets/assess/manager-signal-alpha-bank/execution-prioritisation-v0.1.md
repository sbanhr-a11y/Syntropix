## EP-01 — Three urgent commitments, one constrained team
Construct: Execution & Prioritisation
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
Your team has capacity to complete only two of three commitments this week:
- a customer issue affecting a major account;
- a leadership-requested analysis due Friday;
- a process improvement expected to reduce recurring errors.

What would you do first?

A. Ask the team to work longer hours so all three can be delivered. **0**
B. Assess consequence, dependency and reversibility of each commitment, then make the trade-off explicit with affected stakeholders. **3**
C. Complete the leadership request first because it came from the most senior stakeholder. **1**
D. Prioritise the customer issue and ignore the other two until next week. **2**

### Stage 2
The customer issue is serious but has a safe temporary workaround. The process error has caused three incidents this month.

A. Use the workaround, prioritise the recurring-error fix, and renegotiate the analysis deadline with evidence. **3**
B. Keep customer work first because customers always take priority. **1**
C. Do the leadership analysis because the workaround removes urgency. **1**
D. Split the team equally across all three. **1**

### Stage 3
Leadership refuses to move the analysis deadline.

A. Hide the process-fix delay from leadership and protect the team. **0**
B. Present the explicit consequence/capacity trade-off and ask for a prioritisation decision if no additional capacity exists. **3**
C. Cancel the process improvement. **1**
D. Ask the team to decide collectively which commitment should slip. **1**

## EP-02 — Launch versus operational incident
Construct: Execution & Prioritisation
Subconstruct: escalation; resource allocation
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
A product launch is scheduled tomorrow. A production issue begins affecting a small but growing number of existing customers. The same specialists are needed for both.

A. Keep the launch team intact unless the incident becomes severe. **1**
B. Establish incident severity/trend, define customer and launch consequences, and reallocate specialists based on risk rather than sunk launch effort. **3**
C. Cancel the launch immediately. **1**
D. Let technical specialists decide without management involvement. **2**

### Stage 2
The issue is not yet severe, but evidence suggests it could spread quickly.

A. Protect the launch because the current impact remains small. **1**
B. Move enough critical expertise to contain/diagnose the incident while defining a go/no-go checkpoint for the launch. **3**
C. Cancel the launch permanently. **0**
D. Continue both at full scope and ask the team to stretch. **0**

### Stage 3
A senior sponsor says launch delay would damage a public commitment.

A. Launch regardless; reputational damage is now the larger risk. **0**
B. Keep the evidence-based go/no-go gate and escalate the reputational trade-off rather than suppressing operational risk. **3**
C. Ask the technical team to sign off so responsibility transfers to them. **0**
D. Delay without informing the sponsor until the incident is resolved. **1**

## EP-03 — Delegation under deadline pressure
Construct: Execution & Prioritisation
Subconstruct: delegation; capacity; risk
Initial CX: CX1
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
You must deliver a board update tomorrow. A capable team member can prepare most of the analysis but has not presented to executives before.

A. Do the analysis yourself because the deadline is too important. **1**
B. Delegate the analysis with a clear output standard and checkpoints, while retaining responsibility for executive synthesis. **3**
C. Delegate the entire board update and let the employee learn through experience. **1**
D. Ask two team members to independently prepare versions so you can choose the best. **0**

### Stage 2
At the checkpoint, the analysis is mostly correct but one conclusion is not sufficiently supported.

A. Take back the work. **1**
B. Explain the evidence gap, ask the employee to correct it and preserve the delegated ownership. **3**
C. Quietly fix the conclusion yourself. **2**
D. Leave it because senior leaders may not notice. **0**

### Stage 3
A last-minute executive request adds a new analysis that cannot be completed reliably before the meeting.

A. Provide an estimate and label it as fact to avoid an incomplete deck. **0**
B. State what can be supported now, identify the missing analysis and commit to a verified follow-up. **3**
C. Remove the topic entirely. **1**
D. Ask the employee to make the best possible estimate without mentioning uncertainty. **0**
