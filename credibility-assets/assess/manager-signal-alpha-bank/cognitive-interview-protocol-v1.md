# Manager Signal — Cognitive Interview Protocol v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: PRE-PILOT EXECUTION PROTOCOL

## Purpose

Test whether practising managers interpret Manager Signal scenarios, options, consequences and challenge-back stages as intended before any psychometric pilot.

This is not a validation study.

## Target sample

Planning range: **18–24 managers**, adjusted based on issue saturation.

Coverage:
- 6 first-line managers
- 8 mid-level managers
- 4–6 senior managers / managers of managers

Across at least:
- commercial/customer-facing
- corporate/support
- technology/product/data
- operations/manufacturing/supply chain

Primary market representation:
- India strongly represented

Where feasible:
- 4–6 participants with multinational/global management exposure.

## Session length

Target:
**45–60 minutes**

Each participant reviews a balanced subset of approximately **4–6 testlets**, not the entire bank.

## Opening script

Explain:

> We are testing the assessment, not you. There is no hiring decision attached to this session. We want to understand how real managers interpret the situations and response options, including where the wording is unrealistic, too obvious, culturally specific or missing important context.

Ask participants to think aloud where comfortable.

## Per-stage interview sequence

### 1. First comprehension

Before options:
- What do you think is happening here?
- What decision does the manager actually need to make?
- Which details matter most?
- What information feels missing?

### 2. Response process

After seeing options:
- Which option would you most likely choose?
- Which option would you choose second?
- Why?
- What trade-off separates your first and second choice?
- Is any option obviously bad or unrealistic?
- Does one option sound “more professional” rather than actually better?
- Is one option longer/more detailed in a way that gives away the expected answer?

### 3. Construct check

Ask:
- What capability do you think this question is testing?
- Could someone score well mainly because of policy knowledge or industry experience?
- Would a strong manager in another function reasonably answer differently?

### 4. Complexity check

For later stages:
- Did the situation actually become more difficult?
- What changed?
- Was the new information important enough to reconsider your decision?
- Did the challenge-back feel like a realistic consequence or an artificial trick?

### 5. Cultural / fairness check

Ask where relevant:
- Would hierarchy norms affect how someone answers?
- Would this feel different in another country or organisation?
- Is any wording culturally loaded?
- Does success depend on corporate experience unrelated to managerial quality?

### 6. AI-integrity check

For AI-era items:
- Does this test responsible managerial judgment or merely knowledge of AI policy?
- Would the best decision still make sense if the AI product changed?
- Is the principle durable enough for a global product?

## Post-session questions

1. Which scenario felt most realistic?
2. Which felt least realistic?
3. Which response set was easiest to “game”?
4. Which scenario forced the most meaningful trade-off?
5. Did any scenario feel like an HR compliance quiz?
6. Did any response sound morally “correct” rather than operationally strong?
7. Was reading burden appropriate?
8. Did the evolving scenarios remain understandable?
9. Would you tolerate approximately 35–40 minutes of this in a real hiring process?
10. What would make the experience feel more premium/respectful?

## Issue coding

Code each issue:
- comprehension
- missing context
- construct contamination
- cueing/social desirability
- unrealistic option
- complexity failure
- cultural dependence
- accessibility
- excessive reading
- challenge-back incoherence
- AI-policy trivia
- other

Severity:
- LOW
- MODERATE
- HIGH
- BLOCKER

## Stop/rewrite criteria

Rewrite before pilot when:
- multiple managers interpret the construct differently;
- the best response is obvious because of tone/length/morality;
- important context is consistently missing;
- experts disagree because company policy rather than judgment determines the answer;
- challenge-back punishes a reasonable earlier path;
- CX3/CX4 complexity is primarily reading burden;
- material cultural differences cannot be handled by adaptation.
