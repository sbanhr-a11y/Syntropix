## AI-01 — Confidential data and generative AI
Construct: AI-era Management Judgment
Subconstruct: privacy; task suitability; accountability
Initial CX: CX1
Peak CX: CX3

### Stage 1
A team member wants to paste a confidential client document into a public AI tool to produce a summary quickly.

A. Allow it if the employee removes the client's name. **0**
B. Stop the upload, use only an approved environment/process, and consider whether the task can be completed without exposing restricted information. **3**
C. Ban the team from all AI tools. **1**
D. Allow it because summarisation is low risk. **0**

### Stage 2
An approved enterprise AI tool is available, but its output may still contain errors.

A. Use the output as the final summary because the tool is approved. **1**
B. Use the approved tool while requiring human verification against the source for consequential statements. **3**
C. Do not use AI because errors are possible. **2**
D. Ask the AI to verify itself. **0**

### Stage 3
The summary will inform a commercial decision the same day.

A. Increase verification of the claims material to the decision and make uncertainty/source boundaries visible. **3**
B. Use it because deadline pressure makes full review impractical. **0**
C. Delay the decision until every sentence can be independently audited. **1**
D. Add a general disclaimer and proceed. **1**

## AI-02 — AI-generated employee performance summary
Construct: AI-era Management Judgment
Subconstruct: human oversight; fairness; sensitive inference
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
Your company provides an approved AI assistant. A manager asks whether they can feed performance notes into it to generate year-end summaries.

A. Encourage it because it will make reviews more consistent. **1**
B. Confirm approved data/use rules, use AI only as drafting support where permitted, and require the manager to verify evidence and own the judgment. **3**
C. Ban AI from performance reviews entirely. **2**
D. Allow it as long as the employee never sees the AI draft. **0**

### Stage 2
The AI describes one employee as “low leadership potential,” although the notes contain no explicit evidence about future potential.

A. Keep the phrase because the model may identify patterns humans miss. **0**
B. Remove/challenge the unsupported inference and restrict the summary to evidence actually present in the approved inputs. **3**
C. Ask the AI for a confidence score. **0**
D. Replace “low” with “moderate” potential. **0**

### Stage 3
Senior leadership wants AI-generated summaries across the company to compare talent more quickly.

A. Support it if the same AI model is used for everyone. **1**
B. Require governance around purpose, evidence, human accountability, fairness and permissible inference before using AI output in consequential talent comparisons. **3**
C. Reject all use because AI should never support people decisions. **2**
D. Use the summaries but prevent managers from seeing how they were generated. **0**

## AI-03 — AI recommendation conflicts with experienced employee
Construct: AI-era Management Judgment
Subconstruct: verification; human override
Initial CX: CX2
Peak CX: CX4

### Stage 1
An approved forecasting tool recommends reducing inventory for a product. An experienced team member strongly disagrees based on recent customer conversations.

A. Follow the AI because it uses more data than one employee. **0**
B. Examine the model inputs/assumptions and the employee's new evidence before deciding. **3**
C. Follow the employee because they understand the market. **1**
D. Average the two recommendations. **0**

### Stage 2
You find the AI model does not include a recent competitor exit that the employee knows about.

A. Override the recommendation and document the missing variable while requesting an updated analysis/model input. **3**
B. Still use the model because one unmodelled event may be temporary. **1**
C. Stop using the forecasting tool permanently. **1**
D. Let the employee decide and accept responsibility. **0**

### Stage 3
The updated model now partially supports the employee's view but with high uncertainty.

A. Make a staged/reversible inventory decision, monitor leading indicators and keep both model and field evidence visible. **3**
B. Follow the employee fully because they were initially right. **1**
C. Follow the updated model mechanically. **1**
D. Delay until uncertainty disappears. **0**
