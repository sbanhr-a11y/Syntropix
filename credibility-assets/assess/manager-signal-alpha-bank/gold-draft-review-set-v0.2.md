Date: 2026-09-23
Status: METHOD REVIEW DRAFT / NOT FOR CANDIDATE USE

## Why this set exists

The first alpha bank intentionally exposed the authoring architecture. Automated review then found unacceptable response-key and option-length cueing. This v0.2 set is the first deliberately re-authored subset using:
- plausible alternatives;
- similar option tone/length;
- varied semantic key positions;
- genuine trade-offs;
- no “virtuous paragraph versus reckless one-liner” pattern.

Candidate rendering should still randomise displayed option order independently from semantic option IDs.

# GD-PJ-01 — High performer, deteriorating team climate

Construct: People Judgment  
CX path: CX2 → CX3 → CX4  
Primary tension: performance continuity vs team harm/accountability

## Stage 1

A strong performer consistently delivers above target. Two team members separately say the person dismisses others' ideas and interrupts colleagues in meetings. You have not personally seen a serious incident.

What would you do first?

A. Observe the next few team meetings yourself before raising the concern, so you can form an independent view. **2**

B. Ask the wider team whether communication has become a problem, then decide whether an individual conversation is necessary. **1**

C. Gather specific examples from the people raising the concern, then discuss the pattern and expectations directly with the employee. **3**

D. Speak with the employee first about how they think the team is functioning, without yet sharing the specific concerns raised. **2**

Why C is currently strongest:
It combines sufficient fact-finding with timely accountability. A and D create useful information but delay direct handling; B broadens disclosure and may create unnecessary team dynamics.

## Stage 2

You learn the employee received similar informal feedback six months ago. They disagree with the concern and point to their strong results.

A. Keep delivery expectations unchanged, define observable conduct expectations, and review both sets of outcomes over a short period. **3**

B. Move more collaborative work to other team members while keeping the employee focused on individual delivery. **1**

C. Ask the employee to propose their own behaviour changes, then review whether colleagues perceive an improvement. **2**

D. Escalate immediately into a formal process because the previous feedback shows the issue has already been addressed. **2**

Why A:
It maintains accountability on both performance and conduct without prematurely separating the employee from team work or assuming informal feedback equals a completed formal process.

## Stage 3

A critical customer asks specifically for this employee on a high-risk project. Your director says changing the team now could threaten the account.

A. Keep the employee on the account, but postpone the conduct plan until the customer milestone has passed. **1**

B. Replace the employee on the account and explain to the customer that an internal staffing issue required the change. **1**

C. Keep the employee involved with tighter operating boundaries, continue the conduct plan, and monitor team/customer risk explicitly. **3**

D. Keep the employee involved and add a second senior colleague to reduce interpersonal risk while you reassess after the milestone. **2**

# GD-EP-01 — Capacity conflict across three commitments

Construct: Execution & Prioritisation  
CX path: CX2 → CX3 → CX3  
Primary tension: urgent visible work vs recurring systemic risk

## Stage 1

Your team can realistically complete only two of these this week:
- a major-customer issue;
- a leadership analysis due Friday;
- a process fix expected to reduce recurring errors.

A. Protect the customer issue and leadership deadline, then schedule the process fix as the first priority next week. **2**

B. Divide available capacity across all three so each commitment shows visible progress. **1**

C. Assess consequence, dependency and reversibility across all three, then make the required trade-off explicit to affected stakeholders. **3**

D. Ask for temporary overtime while you wait to see which issue becomes most urgent. **1**

## Stage 2

The customer has a safe temporary workaround. The process issue has caused three incidents this month.

A. Keep the customer issue first because temporary workarounds create reputational risk if they persist. **2**

B. Prioritise the recurring-error fix, use the customer workaround temporarily, and renegotiate the analysis deadline with evidence. **3**

C. Complete the leadership analysis and process fix, then return to the customer issue before the workaround expires. **2**

D. Split one specialist onto each priority and reserve the remaining capacity for whichever problem worsens first. **1**

## Stage 3

Leadership says the analysis deadline cannot move.

A. Present the capacity and consequence trade-off, then ask the accountable leader to choose if no additional resource is available. **3**

B. Preserve all three commitments by reducing scope in each and informing stakeholders that final quality may be lower. **1**

C. Keep the process fix and customer workaround, then deliver a shorter analysis using only currently verified information. **2**

D. Complete the analysis as requested and document that the process-fix risk was accepted by leadership. **2**

# GD-DE-01 — Conflicting retention evidence

Construct: Decision & Evidence Quality  
CX path: CX2 → CX3 → CX3  
Primary tension: official metric vs faster leading evidence

## Stage 1

Two dashboards report materially different customer-retention figures. Leadership wants a recommendation today.

A. Use the official dashboard because governance requires one source of truth for management decisions. **2**

B. Use the lower number as the safer assumption until the systems can be reconciled. **1**

C. Identify the definition/source differences most likely to explain the gap before basing the recommendation on either number. **3**

D. Present the range between both dashboards and avoid choosing between them until the next reporting cycle. **2**

## Stage 2

You learn one dashboard updates daily, while the official dashboard is monthly and uses a slightly different definition.

A. Replace the monthly measure with the daily one for this decision because it is more current. **1**

B. Use the official measure for reported performance and the daily measure as a leading risk signal, explaining their different purposes. **3**

C. Continue with the official metric but add a note that a second system currently shows a lower result. **2**

D. Blend the two measures into a temporary midpoint so the recommendation reflects both sources. **1**

## Stage 3

The daily measure now shows a sharp deterioration that the monthly metric cannot yet reflect.

A. Begin proportionate investigation/action based on the leading signal while making its uncertainty and definition visible. **3**

B. Wait for the official metric before taking action, but prepare contingency plans in case the decline is confirmed. **2**

C. Treat the daily movement as evidence that retention has fallen and immediately revise the quarterly forecast. **1**

D. Ask the dashboard owner to certify the daily number before any operational action is taken. **2**

# GD-SJ-01 — Senior request to bypass a control

Construct: Stakeholder Judgment  
CX path: CX2 → CX3 → CX4  
Primary tension: urgency/commercial pressure vs governed risk

## Stage 1

A senior leader asks you to bypass a standard approval because the matter is urgent and “everyone already agrees.”

A. Use the exception this once, document the leader's instruction, and return to normal process afterward. **1**

B. Clarify why the control exists and identify whether an authorised fast-track or exception route can meet the urgency. **3**

C. Refuse to bypass the process, but offer to personally accelerate each approval step. **2**

D. Ask the senior leader to confirm the request in writing before deciding whether to proceed. **2**

## Stage 2

You learn the control was introduced after previous exceptions created customer risk.

A. Use the authorised escalation path and present both urgency and customer-risk implications to the decision authority. **3**

B. Follow the standard process without exception because the historical risk now makes the trade-off clear. **2**

C. Propose a temporary exception with additional monitoring so speed is preserved while some control is retained. **2**

D. Ask the senior leader to accept accountability formally if they still want the control bypassed. **1**

## Stage 3

The leader says the delay could cost a major client and asks you to “own the commercial consequence.”

A. Escalate the combined commercial/control-risk decision to the authority empowered to accept both consequences. **3**

B. Use the fast-track route if available and accept a higher level of monitoring after the decision. **2**

C. Follow the control strictly and document that the commercial consequence resulted from governance requirements. **1**

D. Make the exception because preserving the client is now the more material business consequence. **1**

# GD-LA-01 — Successful strategy stops working

Construct: Learning & Adaptive Judgment  
CX path: CX2 → CX3 → CX4  
Primary tension: persistence vs evidence-led adaptation

## Stage 1

A strategy you introduced worked well for two quarters. Results have declined for six weeks.

A. Continue for one more cycle because six weeks may be too short to distinguish a real shift from normal variation. **2**

B. Review whether execution, conditions or original assumptions changed before deciding whether persistence or adjustment is justified. **3**

C. Ask the team to identify two alternative strategies while continuing the current approach unchanged. **2**

D. Increase execution discipline first because the strategy has already demonstrated that it can work. **1**

## Stage 2

Execution quality remained stable, but customer behaviour changed materially.

A. Modify the strategy around the changed behaviour and define leading indicators that would confirm or challenge the revision. **3**

B. Continue the current strategy while testing a small alternative with one customer segment. **2**

C. Increase customer incentives so the original strategy remains viable under the new behaviour. **1**

D. Reduce the target temporarily until enough data exists to determine whether customer behaviour has changed permanently. **2**

## Stage 3

Senior leadership publicly praised the original strategy, and it is associated with your reputation.

A. Present the changed evidence and recommend revision while retaining only elements still supported by current conditions. **3**

B. Introduce adjustments gradually without describing them as a strategy change until results improve. **2**

C. Continue the current approach for one additional reporting period to avoid reversing a recently endorsed strategy prematurely. **1**

D. Ask a peer leader to review the data independently before you recommend changing your own strategy. **2**

# GD-AI-01 — AI-generated performance summary

Construct: AI-era Management Judgment  
CX path: CX2 → CX3 → CX4  
Primary tension: efficiency vs evidence/fairness/accountability

## Stage 1

Your company provides an approved AI assistant. A manager wants to use it to turn documented performance notes into year-end review drafts.

A. Allow the use if the manager verifies the final text and remains accountable for the review. **2**

B. Allow the use only after confirming the approved data/use policy, with AI limited to drafting and evidence verification retained by the manager. **3**

C. Keep AI out of performance reviews because employment decisions require exclusively human-authored documentation. **2**

D. Allow the use only for employees who consent to AI-assisted drafting of their review. **1**

## Stage 2

The AI draft describes one employee as “low leadership potential,” although the source notes contain no explicit evidence about future potential.

A. Ask the manager whether their own experience supports the conclusion before deciding whether to retain it. **2**

B. Remove the unsupported inference and restrict the review to claims that can be traced to relevant evidence. **3**

C. Ask the AI to explain which observations led to the potential inference, then retain it if the explanation appears coherent. **1**

D. Reframe the statement as a development hypothesis and discuss it with the employee rather than treating it as a conclusion. **2**

## Stage 3

Senior leadership proposes using AI-generated performance summaries across the company to accelerate talent comparisons.

A. Use a common AI template for consistency, but require every manager to sign off before summaries enter talent review. **2**

B. Establish purpose, permissible evidence, human-accountability, fairness and audit controls before AI summaries influence consequential comparisons. **3**

C. Permit summaries only as meeting-preparation notes and prohibit them from being stored with formal talent decisions. **2**

D. Delay expansion until an external AI vendor can provide a fairness certification for the model. **1**
