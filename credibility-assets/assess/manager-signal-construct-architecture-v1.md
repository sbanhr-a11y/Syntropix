# Syntropix Manager Signal — Construct Architecture v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: CONSTRUCT FREEZE CANDIDATE / PRE-ITEM BUILD
Product: Syntropix Manager Signal
Target experience: ~35–40 minutes

## Product question

**How does this candidate exercise managerial judgment when people, priorities, evidence, stakeholders and technology create competing demands?**

Manager Signal is not intended to diagnose personality, intelligence, mental health, leadership “DNA,” or cultural fit. It is a job-related decision-evidence assessment.

## Evidence method

Primary method:
- stateful situational judgment testlets;
- shorter decisionlets;
- role-context evidence;
- AI-era management situations;
- challenge-back items.

The assessment should emphasise observable decisions over self-description.

## Construct 1 — People Judgment

### Definition
Quality of judgment when a manager must balance individual needs, team functioning, performance expectations, accountability and fair process.

### Included content
- performance conversations;
- feedback/coaching;
- delegation;
- conflict;
- recognition;
- underperformance;
- employee concerns;
- psychological safety in practical manager behaviour;
- consistency/fairness of process;
- escalation where appropriate.

### Exclusions
Not:
- empathy as a personality trait;
- clinical emotional intelligence;
- charisma;
- “likeability”;
- agreement with employees;
- popularity.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence generally reflects:
- clarifying facts before action;
- proportional response;
- explicit performance expectations;
- accountability without humiliation;
- appropriate coaching/support;
- fair and consistent process;
- recognition of team-level consequences;
- escalation when authority/risk requires it.

### Key tensions
Performance vs support
Individual vs team
Speed vs process
Autonomy vs oversight
Confidentiality vs escalation

## Construct 2 — Execution & Prioritisation Judgment

### Definition
Quality of decisions about sequencing work, allocating constrained resources, protecting critical outcomes and adapting execution as conditions change.

### Included content
- competing priorities;
- resource scarcity;
- sequencing;
- deadlines;
- dependencies;
- escalation;
- reprioritisation;
- delegation;
- operational follow-through.

### Exclusions
Not:
- conscientiousness questionnaire;
- busyness;
- willingness to work excessive hours;
- speed for its own sake.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence generally reflects:
- distinction between urgent and important;
- explicit trade-offs;
- protection of critical dependencies;
- realistic allocation of capacity;
- appropriate delegation;
- timely escalation;
- revision when assumptions change.

## Construct 3 — Decision & Evidence Quality

### Definition
Quality of reasoning when evidence is incomplete, conflicting, uncertain or changing.

### Included content
- fact vs assumption;
- evidence quality;
- data interpretation;
- disconfirming information;
- uncertainty;
- reversibility;
- risk;
- decision thresholds;
- updating decisions.

### Exclusions
Not:
- general IQ;
- abstract intelligence label;
- perfect numerical ability;
- “confidence.”

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence generally reflects:
- identifying what is known/unknown;
- seeking decision-relevant information;
- avoiding false precision;
- distinguishing reversible from irreversible decisions;
- appropriately weighting evidence;
- changing course when new evidence warrants it.

## Construct 4 — Stakeholder Judgment

### Definition
Quality of judgment when legitimate stakeholder interests differ and influence must be exercised without losing role accountability.

### Included content
- cross-functional conflict;
- senior stakeholder pressure;
- customer tension;
- boundary negotiation;
- expectation management;
- escalation;
- influence;
- communication sequencing.

### Exclusions
Not:
- extraversion;
- persuasion style;
- political manipulation;
- popularity.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence generally reflects:
- understanding stakeholder interests;
- preserving clarity of accountability;
- surfacing trade-offs;
- choosing appropriate channel/timing;
- avoiding unnecessary escalation;
- escalating when mandate/risk requires it;
- maintaining decision integrity under hierarchy pressure.

## Construct 5 — Learning & Adaptive Judgment

### Definition
Quality of response when previous assumptions, plans or behaviours no longer fit the situation.

### Included content
- feedback response;
- changed constraints;
- failed assumptions;
- unexpected outcomes;
- new evidence;
- unfamiliar situations;
- correction of course.

### Exclusions
Not:
- self-reported learning agility;
- novelty seeking;
- agreeableness toward feedback;
- change enthusiasm.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence generally reflects:
- noticing evidence that a prior approach is not working;
- separating ego from decision revision;
- preserving useful prior learning without rigidly applying it;
- testing revised approaches;
- learning from consequences.

## Construct 6 — AI-era Management Judgment

### Definition
Quality of managerial decisions about where AI can appropriately assist work and where human verification, privacy, accountability or escalation is required.

### Included content
- task delegation to AI;
- verification;
- hallucination/unsupported output risk;
- confidential information;
- human oversight;
- employee AI use;
- AI-assisted decisions;
- transparency;
- escalation;
- accountability.

### Exclusions
Not:
- prompt-engineering trivia;
- knowledge of model brands;
- technical AI expertise;
- enthusiasm for AI.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence generally reflects:
- choosing AI use based on task/risk;
- verifying consequential outputs;
- protecting confidential data;
- maintaining accountable human ownership;
- recognising unsupported claims;
- not automating high-consequence judgment merely for speed.

## Embedded design principles — not standalone scores

The following should be designed across scenarios rather than presented as easy-to-game constructs:
- fairness;
- ethics/integrity;
- inclusion;
- communication clarity;
- customer orientation;
- commercial awareness;
- psychological safety;
- ownership.

These can appear as observable evidence patterns or job-family overlays when justified.

## Complexity architecture

All constructs must span contextual complexity:
- CX1 Clear Context
- CX2 Competing Priorities
- CX3 Ambiguous Trade-offs
- CX4 High-Consequence Complexity

Default Manager Signal design mix remains a hypothesis:
- CX1 10%
- CX2 35%
- CX3 40%
- CX4 15%

Role level and job-family relevance may adjust the distribution.

## Job-family overlays

The six constructs remain common. Job-family lenses change:
- situation content;
- stakeholders;
- vocabulary;
- decision pressures;
- consequences;
- relevant risks;
- AI usage.

They do not create separate psychometric constructs without evidence.

No automatic Hire / Reject.

## Research foundation

Design is informed by:
- personnel-selection evidence favouring job-related methods;
- structured interview/job-analysis principles;
- SJT evidence for managerial/interpersonal/problem-solving constructs;
- applicant-reaction research favouring job-relevant opportunity-to-perform methods;
- current research on candidate GenAI use and AI-enabled assessment design.

External literature informs design. It does not validate this Syntropix instrument.
