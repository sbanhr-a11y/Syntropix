# Syntropix Campus Signal — Construct Architecture v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: CONSTRUCT FREEZE CANDIDATE / PRE-ITEM BUILD
Product: Syntropix Campus Signal
Target experience: ~35–40 minutes
Target population: adult early-career / graduate candidates, initially 18+

## Product question

**What job-relevant evidence does this candidate demonstrate of reasoning, learning, workplace judgment and responsible AI-era work readiness before extensive work experience can tell the story?**

Campus Signal must not reward prestige, expensive prior coaching or hidden professional experience.

## Construct 1 — Applied Reasoning

### Definition
Ability to interpret job-relevant information, distinguish supported from unsupported conclusions, and make defensible decisions using the evidence provided.

### Included content
- tables/charts;
- short written evidence;
- numerical interpretation;
- verbal inference;
- simple business constraints;
- competing evidence;
- logic applied to realistic work information.

### Exclusions
Not:
- abstract IQ branding;
- obscure puzzles;
- advanced mathematics unrelated to target work;
- vocabulary prestige;
- speed as a proxy for intelligence.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence reflects:
- correct interpretation;
- appropriate inference;
- identifying insufficient evidence;
- avoiding unsupported conclusions;
- selecting relevant information.

## Construct 2 — Learning Transfer

### Definition
Ability to understand a novel rule/system presented inside the assessment and apply that newly learned information accurately when the context changes.

### Included content
- fictional policies;
- synthetic workflows;
- unfamiliar categorisation systems;
- new operating rules;
- changed examples;
- transfer to novel cases.

### Exclusions
Not:
- self-reported learning agility;
- prior domain knowledge;
- academic-memory trivia.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence reflects:
- accurate rule acquisition;
- combination of rules;
- transfer beyond worked examples;
- recognition of exceptions;
- revision when new rules/evidence are introduced.

This construct is particularly important because it allows observable evidence without requiring extensive prior work experience.

## Construct 3 — Workplace Judgment

### Definition
Quality of decisions in common early-career workplace situations involving priorities, collaboration, accountability, escalation and professional conduct.

### Included content
- competing tasks;
- unclear instructions;
- mistakes;
- team coordination;
- deadlines;
- requesting help;
- escalation;
- customer/colleague interactions;
- responsibility for errors.

### Exclusions
Not:
- knowledge of hidden corporate etiquette;
- “culture fit”;
- socioeconomic familiarity with office life;
- personality labels.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence reflects:
- proportionate action;
- appropriate help-seeking;
- clear ownership;
- respectful collaboration;
- sensible escalation;
- protection of critical outcomes.

## Construct 4 — Communication Judgment

### Definition
Ability to choose what information should be communicated, to whom, when, and at what level of detail in a work situation.

### Included content
- audience;
- channel;
- sequencing;
- clarity;
- escalation;
- concise written choices;
- identifying missing context.

### Exclusions
Not:
- accent;
- ornamental English;
- charisma;
- native-speaker style;
- personality-based sociability.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence reflects:
- audience-fit;
- clarity;
- appropriate detail;
- timely escalation;
- distinction between fact and assumption;
- avoidance of unnecessary noise.

## Construct 5 — Ownership & Reliability Judgment

### Definition
Quality of decisions concerning commitments, errors, follow-through, responsibility and predictable delivery.

### Included content
- missed deadlines;
- mistakes;
- handoffs;
- dependencies;
- commitments;
- transparency;
- recovery actions.

### Exclusions
Not:
- self-report statements such as “I am dependable”;
- willingness to work unhealthy hours;
- obedience.

### Evidence logic
Strong evidence reflects:
- early visibility of risk;
- ownership of mistakes;
- realistic commitments;
- follow-through;
- escalation before failure becomes avoidable harm.

## Construct 6 — AI Work Judgment

### Definition
Quality of decisions about appropriate AI assistance, output verification, confidentiality, source reliability and human accountability in early-career work.

### Included content
- AI-generated work;
- unsupported output;
- confidential data;
- fact/source checking;
- disclosure;
- task appropriateness;
- human review.

### Exclusions
Not:
- AI-product familiarity;
- coding AI models;
- prompt tricks;
- enthusiasm for AI.

## Potential construct consolidation to test

Workplace Judgment, Communication Judgment, and Ownership/Reliability may prove empirically overlapping. They remain separate design domains initially so item content can be intentionally sampled, but the factor structure must be determined empirically rather than assumed.

## Complexity architecture

Campus must use:
- CX1 Clear Context
- CX2 Competing Priorities
- CX3 Ambiguous Trade-offs
- CX4 High-Consequence Complexity

Default design hypothesis:
- CX1 15%
- CX2 40%
- CX3 35%
- CX4 10%

Campus CX4 must be difficult because of novel information/trade-offs, not prior professional knowledge.

## Candidate-experience fairness principles

Do not rely on:
- college brand;
- GPA as a scored construct;
- accent;
- socioeconomic proxies;
- unexplained business jargon;
- inaccessible time pressure.

Current graduate-selection research raises meaningful fairness questions about heavy early-stage cognitive testing. Applied reasoning should therefore be job-relevant, proportionate, and only one part of the assessment.

## Research foundation

Current employer research shows increasing use of skills-based hiring for entry-level candidates and increasing demand for candidates to demonstrate skills. Applicant-reaction evidence also favours job-related opportunity-to-perform methods.

External literature informs design. It does not validate Campus Signal.
