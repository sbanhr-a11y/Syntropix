# Campus Signal — Cognitive Interview Protocol v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: PRE-PILOT EXECUTION PROTOCOL

## Purpose

Test whether adult early-career candidates understand Campus Signal as intended, whether the experience is equitable across different educational backgrounds, and whether Learn→Apply measures application rather than hidden business knowledge or memory.

## Target sample

Planning range: **24–30 adults aged 18+**, adjusted based on issue saturation.

Coverage should intentionally include:
- final-year students
- recent graduates
- 0–2 year professionals
- public/state universities
- private universities
- non-elite colleges
- Tier 1/2/3 city backgrounds where feasible
- different academic streams
- candidates with and without internship experience

Do not recruit only MBA/engineering graduates.

Include:
- candidates educated primarily in English
- candidates for whom English is a second/third language but who meet the intended product's English-use requirement.

## Session length

Target:
**45–60 minutes**

Use only a subset of items plus Learn→Apply to avoid fatigue.

## Opening script

> We are testing the assessment, not your employability. Your answers will not be used for hiring. We want to know whether the tasks are understandable, fair and relevant to early-career work, and whether any question gives an advantage simply because someone has more corporate exposure.

## Applied Reasoning probes

For each:
- What information did you use?
- Was any calculation difficult because of mathematics rather than reasoning?
- Did any wording require business knowledge?
- Did the answer feel obvious from option length/tone?
- Could someone with a different educational background understand the task?

## Workplace Judgment probes

- Have you encountered something similar?
- If not, was there still enough information to decide?
- Does this assume prior office/internship knowledge?
- Which two options felt most plausible?
- Was one answer obviously “professional sounding”?
- Would this situation be interpreted differently in your cultural/educational context?

## Communication / Ownership probes

- Is the strongest response about judgment, or about knowing corporate etiquette?
- Are communication expectations clear without requiring polished English?
- Does the item reward unnecessary deference to managers/senior leaders?

## AI Work Judgment probes

- Is the situation understandable regardless of which AI product you use?
- Does it test verification/privacy/accountability rather than AI jargon?
- Do you understand what “approved AI tool” means in context?
- Would your answer change depending on employer policy that was not provided?

## Learn→Apply protocol

### Phase 1 — rule learning
Observe:
- reading time;
- rereading;
- questions asked;
- misunderstood terms.

Ask:
- What do you think Routine / Priority / Incident mean?
- Can you explain the precedence rule in your own words?
- What does “Secure Review first” mean?

### Phase 2 — first five applications
After each:
- Which rule did you use?
- Did you need to look back?
- Was looking back useful rather than frustrating?

### Phase 3 — Peak Window update
Ask:
- What changed?
- How does the new rule interact with the old rules?
- Did the “New” rule marker help?

### Phase 4 — challenge-back
Ask:
- Why did your earlier decision need to stay/change?
- Did the new information genuinely alter the rule?

## Key research decision: reference visibility

Test two user-experience variants during cognitive interviews:

### Variant A
Rules always visible/collapsible.

### Variant B
Rules accessible through a reference button requiring one action to reopen.

Do **not** test a hidden-memory version at this stage.

Record:
- completion time;
- reread frequency;
- perceived cognitive burden;
- error patterns.

## Candidate-experience questions

1. Did this feel like an academic exam or a work challenge?
2. Which section felt most relevant to real work?
3. Which felt least fair?
4. Did you feel college/internship background gave an advantage?
5. Did timing feel reasonable?
6. Would you complete a 35–40 minute version for a job you genuinely wanted?
7. What would make you abandon it?
8. Which information about purpose/privacy/AI rules would you need before starting?
9. Did the interface/content feel respectful rather than “testing intelligence”?
10. What accommodation would make the task more accessible?
