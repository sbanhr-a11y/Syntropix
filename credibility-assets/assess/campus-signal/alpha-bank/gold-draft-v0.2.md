## GD-AR-01 — Process speed versus quality

Construct: Applied Reasoning  
CX: CX2

A fictional team changed a process. Average handling time fell from 18 minutes to 13 minutes, while rework rose from 4% to 9%.

Which conclusion is best supported?

A. The change improved speed but may have reduced quality, so both effects should be considered together. **3**

B. The change was beneficial overall because handling time improved by more than rework increased. **1**

C. The change should be reversed because rework more than doubled after implementation. **1**

D. The evidence is inconclusive until customer satisfaction and staffing levels are also available. **2**

## GD-AR-02 — Training and confidence

Construct: Applied Reasoning  
CX: CX2

Employees who voluntarily attended an optional training session reported higher confidence afterward than employees who did not attend.

Which conclusion is strongest?

A. The training likely increased confidence because the trained group reported better results afterward. **1**

B. The training appears useful, but a larger sample is needed before any conclusion can be made. **2**

C. The trained group reported higher confidence, but voluntary participation means the training's causal effect is uncertain. **3**

D. More confident employees probably chose the training, so the session itself likely had little effect. **1**

## GD-WJ-01 — Conflicting deadlines

Construct: Workplace Judgment  
CX: CX2

Your manager assigned a task due today. Later, a project lead gives you another task due today. You realise both cannot be completed to the expected standard.

What would you do?

A. Prioritise the task from your direct manager and explain the delay to the project lead afterward. **1**

B. Work on both in parallel until it becomes clearer which deadline carries the greater consequence. **2**

C. Ask a colleague to take part of one task so both commitments can remain unchanged. **1**

D. Make the conflict visible early, explain the impact of each delay, and get the priority resolved. **3**

## GD-WJ-03 — Repeated late handoff

Construct: Ownership & Reliability Judgment  
CX: CX3

Your work depends on a teammate's input. They have delivered it late twice, forcing you to rush. Another deadline is approaching.

What would you do?

A. Build extra buffer into your schedule this time so the dependency is less likely to affect your delivery. **2**

## GD-AI-01 — Unsupported AI statistic

Construct: AI Work Judgment  
CX: CX2

An approved AI assistant drafts a presentation and includes the statement, “72% of customers prefer this option.” No source is shown.

What would you do?

A. Keep the figure but label it as an AI-generated estimate until the exact source can be confirmed later. **1**

B. Replace the number with a qualitative phrase such as “many customers” so the slide no longer overstates precision. **1**

C. Ask the AI for the source, then use the claim if the citation it provides appears relevant and credible. **2**

D. Verify the claim against an appropriate source before using it, or remove it if support cannot be established. **3**

## GD-AI-02 — AI recommendation conflicts with yours

Construct: AI Work Judgment  
CX: CX2

You draft a recommendation. An approved AI assistant produces a more polished answer but recommends a different course of action.

What would you do?

A. Compare the assumptions and evidence behind both recommendations, verify material claims, and decide from the stronger reasoning. **3**

B. Keep your own recommendation because you understand the context better than the AI system does. **1**

C. Use the AI recommendation because it can consider more information and patterns than one person can. **0**

D. Combine the two recommendations so the final version reflects both human and AI perspectives. **1**

## GD-AI-03 — AI changes commitment language

Construct: AI Work Judgment  
CX: CX2

You ask AI to shorten a customer email. It changes “we expect to deliver Friday” to “we will deliver Friday.”

What would you do?

A. Send the shorter version because the revised wording is clearer and more confident for the customer. **0**

B. Keep the stronger wording but add “subject to change” so the email remains accurate if delivery slips. **1**

C. Restore the original level of certainty and review the rest of the AI edit for other changes in meaning. **3**

D. Ask the project owner whether Friday is certain enough before deciding which version of the phrase to use. **2**
