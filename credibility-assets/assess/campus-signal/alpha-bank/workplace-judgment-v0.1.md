## WJ-01 — Two deadlines collide
Construct: Workplace Judgment
CX: CX2

You have two tasks due today. Your manager assigned one; a project lead later assigned another. You now realise both cannot be completed to the expected standard by the deadlines.

What would you do?

A. Finish the manager's task first because formal reporting lines should determine priority. **2**
B. Work on both until one becomes clearly more urgent. **1**
C. Make the conflict visible early, clarify impact/dependencies with the relevant people, and agree priority rather than silently missing one. **3**
D. Ask a colleague to complete part of one task even if they were not assigned to it. **1**

## WJ-02 — You discover your mistake
Construct: Ownership & Reliability Judgment
CX: CX2

You realise that a spreadsheet you sent yesterday contains an error. No one has mentioned it yet.

A. Correct the file and replace it in the shared folder without drawing attention to the mistake. **1**
B. Assess who may have used the incorrect information, notify the relevant people promptly, provide the corrected version, and explain any impact. **3**
C. Wait to see whether anyone relied on the error before escalating it. **0**
D. Tell your manager first and let them decide whether others need to know. **2**

## WJ-03 — Unclear instructions
Construct: Workplace Judgment
CX: CX1

A colleague asks you to prepare “the usual customer summary,” but you have never created one and the colleague goes into a meeting.

A. Create what you think a useful customer summary should contain. **2**
B. Wait until the colleague is available again. **1**
C. Look for a previous example or documented template, then clarify only what remains uncertain. **3**
D. Ask several coworkers what they normally include and combine their suggestions. **2**

## WJ-04 — Team member asks for help near your deadline
Construct: Workplace Judgment
CX: CX2

You have a deadline in two hours. A teammate asks for help with a task they need to complete today.

A. Stop your work and help because teamwork should take priority when someone asks. **1**
B. Refuse because your own deadline is closer. **1**
C. Clarify how urgent their issue is and whether a short intervention, alternative resource, or later help can solve it without putting your own critical deadline at risk. **3**
D. Tell them to ask the manager to decide whose work matters more. **2**

## WJ-05 — Customer asks for an answer you do not know
Construct: Communication Judgment
CX: CX2

A customer asks you a technical question. You are not confident that the answer you remember is correct.

A. Give your best answer and say you will confirm afterward. **1**
B. Say you do not know and transfer the customer immediately to someone more experienced. **2**
C. Explain that you want to verify the detail, give a clear follow-up time, and use an appropriate source/person to confirm before answering. **3**
D. Search online quickly and use the first credible-looking answer so the customer is not kept waiting. **0**

## WJ-06 — Repeated late handoff
Construct: Ownership & Reliability Judgment
CX: CX3

Your work depends on another teammate's input. They have delivered it late twice, causing you to rush. The next deadline is approaching.

A. Build extra buffer into your own schedule and avoid creating conflict. **2**
B. Ask the teammate to send the input earlier this time without discussing the previous delays. **2**
C. Discuss the recurring dependency directly, agree a realistic handoff time, and make the risk visible early if the commitment is unlikely to be met. **3**
D. Escalate the teammate to the manager now because the pattern has repeated. **1**
