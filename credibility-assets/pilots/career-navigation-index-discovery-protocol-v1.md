# Career Navigation Index — Discovery Pilot Protocol v1

Study ID: SX-ST-CNI-DISC-2026-01
Instrument: Career Navigation Index
Instrument version: 1.0.0
Scoring version: Suite v2 / assessment-validation-v2
Study role: DISCOVERY
Status: DRAFT / PRE-RECRUITMENT
Date: 2026-09-23

## 1. Purpose

Evaluate whether the current 12-item Career Navigation Index provides a defensible developmental representation of four intended dimensions:
- concern;
- control;
- curiosity;
- confidence.

The study is designed to inform item/structure decisions for Syntropix version 1.0.0. It is not designed to establish population norms, employment-selection utility, causal effects or global generalizability.

## 2. Construct basis

The four intended dimensions are informed by the career-adaptability literature, particularly work describing concern, control, curiosity and confidence as psychosocial resources for navigating career tasks and transitions.

Relevant external evidence includes:
- Savickas & Porfeli (2012), Career Adapt-Abilities Scale: construction, reliability and measurement equivalence across 13 countries, Journal of Vocational Behavior, DOI 10.1016/j.jvb.2012.01.011.
- Porfeli & Savickas (2012), CAAS-USA form, DOI 10.1016/j.jvb.2012.01.009.
- Rudolph, Lavigne & Zacher (2017), meta-analysis of career adaptability and adaptation outcomes, DOI 10.1016/j.jvb.2016.09.002.
- Rudolph, Lavigne, Katz & Zacher (2017), dimension-level meta-analysis, DOI 10.1016/j.jvb.2017.06.003.

These sources support the relevance of the construct family. They do **not** validate Syntropix items, scoring or report thresholds.

## 3. Intended population for this discovery study

Initial population:
- adults 18+;
- English-reading;
- currently employed, self-employed, seeking work, studying toward a career transition, or within a recent career transition;
- initial recruitment primarily in India.

This pilot will be reported as an India/English discovery sample unless the achieved sample genuinely supports a broader description.

## 4. Recruitment strata

Aim for useful heterogeneity across:
- early career;
- mid-career;
- senior/experienced professionals;
- active job/career transition vs relatively stable career context;
- gender groups where voluntarily reported;
- major work sectors where feasible.

Strata are for coverage and exploratory comparability, not quota-based norm construction.

## 5. Pre-pilot qualitative work

Before large-scale recruitment:

### Expert content review
Recommended: 4–6 reviewers spanning:
- vocational/career psychology or career counselling;
- organisational/HR development;
- psychometrics/measurement;
- plain-language/accessibility review.

Review relevance, clarity, redundancy, cultural fit, social desirability and construct contamination.

### Cognitive interviews
Planning target: approximately 15–24 participants across career stages and transition contexts, adjusted based on issue saturation.

Probe:
- interpretation of “career planning,” ownership, exploration and confidence;
- whether “career” is interpreted differently by salaried, entrepreneurial and non-linear workers;
- whether control items accidentally imply unlimited personal agency despite structural constraints;
- whether confidence items are mistaken for general self-esteem;
- whether reverse-keyed items create negation confusion.

Any meaning-changing item revision moves the instrument to at least MINOR version under the change-control standard and the discovery pilot should use the revised frozen version.

## 6. Discovery sample target

Planning target:
- recruit enough participants to yield approximately **300 usable complete responses** after predefined exclusions;
- recruit above that target to allow for incomplete/low-quality cases.

This is a conservative planning target, not a universal psychometric law. Final adequacy will be judged using response quality, communalities/loadings, model stability and the analyses actually attempted.

A future confirmatory study must use an independent sample.

## 7. Data collected

Required:
- 12 CNI item responses;
- instrument/scoring/version identifiers;
- consent version;
- completion timestamp;
- study participant ID.

Protocol-justified context variables:
- age band;
- career stage;
- current transition status;
- employment status;
- broad industry;
- broad geography;
- gender only if included for planned fairness/comparability analysis.

Direct identifiers remain outside the analysis dataset.

## 8. Data-quality rules

Predefine before outcome review:
- duplicate/bot detection;
- incomplete-response exclusion;
- implausibly fast completion rule based on pilot timing distribution, not an arbitrary fixed cutoff;
- repeated response/straight-line flags;
- technical-error exclusions.

Flagged cases may be sensitivity-tested rather than automatically removed when the behaviour could be substantively plausible.

## 9. Primary psychometric analyses

### Item evidence
For each item:
- response distribution;
- missingness;
- floor/ceiling concentration;
- corrected item-rest association within intended dimension;
- inter-item patterns;
- reverse-item behaviour.

### Reliability
Report:
- omega where appropriate as primary;
- alpha as supplementary/comparability;
- uncertainty intervals where feasible.

Do not delete items solely to maximize reliability.

### Internal structure
Because the intended model has four correlated factors with three items each:
- inspect ordinal correlations;
- evaluate the proposed four-factor correlated model using an estimator appropriate to ordinal responses;
- compare against plausible alternatives, including a one-factor model and other theoretically defensible structures;
- inspect loadings, factor correlations, residual/local-dependence signals and Heywood/improper solutions.

Use fit indices as evidence, not mechanical pass/fail rules.

This discovery sample may be used for exploratory model refinement. A revised structure must be frozen and tested in an independent confirmation sample.

## 10. External-variable evidence — discovery

Candidate external measures, subject to licensing/permission and final preregistration:
- an established career-adaptability measure for convergent evidence;
- career planning/exploration measures;
- career decision self-efficacy;
- future orientation or proactive career behaviour;
- a conceptually distinct construct for discriminant evidence.

Do not copy proprietary comparator items into Syntropix.

Primary expectation: CNI scores should show theoretically coherent, non-perfect associations with related career-adaptation constructs. Exact hypotheses/effect directions must be frozen before analysis of those variables.

## 11. Known conceptual risks

- Concern/control/curiosity/confidence may be highly correlated and could behave more like a higher-order construct than four clean short subscales.
- Three items per dimension provide limited redundancy and can make factor solutions sensitive to individual items.
- Self-report introduces common-method and self-presentation effects.
- Control can be confounded with structural privilege/opportunity if wording over-attributes career outcomes to individual agency.
- Confidence must remain career-navigation-specific rather than becoming a general self-worth measure.
- India/English findings must not be described as global evidence.

## 12. Fairness/comparability

Exploratory group analyses may examine:
- gender;
- career stage;
- transition status.

Do not perform or publish strong invariance/fairness conclusions if subgroup n is inadequate.

A later multi-country/language study is required before global comparability claims.

## 13. No-go claims from this study

Even a strong discovery result will **not** support:
- “scientifically proven”;
- population percentiles/norms;
- prediction of promotion, salary or job performance;
- selection/hiring use;
- bias-free claims;
- causal career outcomes;
- global/cross-cultural equivalence.

## 14. Decision after discovery

Possible decisions:
- retain 1.0.0 and proceed to independent confirmation;
- revise one/more items → new MINOR version and restart relevant evidence;
- revise dimension architecture → MAJOR version;
- pause/retire if construct representation cannot be defended.

## 15. Confirmation gate

Do not call the structure confirmed until a frozen post-discovery version is tested in an independent sample with a preregistered model and analysis plan.
