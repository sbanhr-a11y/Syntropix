# Career Navigation Index — Cognitive Interview Sampling Matrix v1

Study: SX-ST-CNI-DISC-2026-01  
Status: PLANNING TEMPLATE

## Goal
Recruit a small, deliberately varied adult sample to expose interpretation problems before large-scale pilot recruitment. This is not intended to be statistically representative.

Planning range: approximately 15–24 interviews, adjusted based on issue saturation.

## Coverage dimensions

### Career stage
Seek coverage across:
- early career / first substantial career choices;
- established mid-career;
- experienced/senior career;
- non-linear or return-to-work pathways.

### Current context
Include participants from several of:
- stable role/no immediate transition;
- active external job search;
- internal role/function transition;
- career break/return;
- self-employment/entrepreneurial path;
- recent redundancy/role loss where recruitment is ethically appropriate and participation is not framed as support/therapy;
- further study/reskilling toward a career move.

### Work environment
Where feasible, vary:
- technology/services;
- manufacturing/operations;
- consumer/retail;
- professional services;
- public/non-profit/education;
- other sectors.

### Demographic/context coverage
Collect only what is justified and consented. Aim for varied:
- gender;
- age bands within 18+;
- geography within the initial India/English scope;
- education pathways.

Do not use quotas to imply norms.

Issue types:
- comprehension;
- retrieval/timeframe;
- response-scale selection;
- social desirability;
- structural-agency assumption;
- cultural/context mismatch;
- reverse-key confusion;
- construct contamination;
- emotional/sensitivity concern;
- other.

## Stop/revise logic

Pause and revise before pilot if:
- multiple participants interpret an item as measuring a different construct;
- a reverse item repeatedly causes direction confusion;
- an item systematically assumes a career context not applicable to the intended population;
- wording implies that structural constraints are personal failure;
- confidence wording is consistently interpreted as general self-worth;
- participants identify materially different meanings across common work contexts.

After any meaning-changing revision, record the semantic-version impact before resuming.
