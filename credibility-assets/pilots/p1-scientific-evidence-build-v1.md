# Syntropix P1 Scientific Evidence Build — Construct & Pilot Architecture v1.0

Status: **PRE-EMPIRICAL / EVIDENCE-BUILDING**  
Effective date: 2026-09-25  
Scope: eight P1 developmental instruments  
Governance boundary: U1/U2 developmental use only unless separately escalated and approved.

## 1. Purpose

This pack converts the eight P1 instruments from “programme initiated” into a controlled scientific evidence-build state.

It does **not** establish reliability, factorial validity, predictive validity, fairness, norms, or consequential-use suitability. It creates the reviewable artefacts needed to test those questions.

Validation follows an interpretation-and-use argument: evidence must support the specific score interpretation and intended use, not the instrument name in the abstract. The programme therefore separates test content, response processes, internal structure, relations with other variables, and consequences/use evidence rather than treating “validation” as a single binary event.

## 2. Shared P1 method

For every P1 instrument:

1. Freeze construct definitions, exclusions, item-to-construct mapping, intended population and use.
2. Conduct independent expert content review.
3. Run cognitive interviews / response-process review.
4. Resolve semantic changes under version control.
5. Freeze the instrument version before pilot analysis.
6. Preregister primary hypotheses, model structure, exclusions, external-variable plan and decision rules.
7. Run a discovery pilot.
8. Treat reliability as score-precision evidence only, never as proof of validity.
9. Separate exploratory model refinement from independent confirmation.
10. Run fairness/comparability work only when subgroup sample sizes and design justify it.

### Shared expert panel
Recommended minimum: 5 independent reviewers, with coverage across:
- psychometrics / psychological measurement;
- organizational or work psychology;
- construct-domain expertise;
- HR/leadership-development practice where relevant;
- accessibility/plain-language/cultural-context review.

No reviewer’s opinion can convert an empirical evidence domain to “supported.”

### Shared cognitive-interview target
Planning target: **15–24 participants per short-form instrument**, sampled for meaningful heterogeneity in seniority, industry, gender where voluntarily reported, and India/English work context. For the 50-item MEI and 200-item ELI, use staged interviewing or item blocks to control fatigue.

### Shared discovery sample policy
- 20–24 item instruments: plan for approximately **300 usable discovery responses**, subject to final model complexity and item behaviour.
- 50-item MEI: plan for **400+ usable discovery responses** because ten subscales and a broader structure must be evaluated.
- 200-item ELI: do **not** launch a conventional full-form discovery pilot until burden, item redundancy and response-process risks have been reviewed. Prefer staged content review, cognitive testing and either a planned matrix-sampling design or a justified larger sample. The 200-item form requires its own power/model-simulation decision before recruitment.
- Independent confirmation sample required after any exploratory refinement.

These are planning values, not universal psychometric thresholds.

# 3. Instrument construct maps

## 3.1 CogniMorph Index™ (CMI)

Instrument version: current Runtime v3 canonical implementation  
Items: 20  
Dimensions: 4 × 5 items

### ALOR — Adaptive Learning Orientation
**Item IDs:** 1, 2R, 3, 4R, 17  
**Intended construct:** self-reported willingness to engage with difficult learning, treat capability as developable, and invest effort in unfamiliar work.  
**External construct families to review:** learning goal orientation; growth mindset / incremental beliefs; learning agility.  
**Explicit exclusions:** general intelligence, cognitive ability, actual learning rate, academic achievement.  
**Primary risks:** socially desirable “learner” identity; conflating persistence with effective strategy change; overstating trainability.

### ERAE — Error Recovery & Experimentation
**Item IDs:** 5, 6R, 7, 8R, 18  
**Intended construct:** tendency to extract information from mistakes, recover after errors, and use failures as inputs to iteration.  
**External construct families to review:** error-management orientation; learning from failure; experimentation/learning behaviour.  
**Explicit exclusions:** emotional resilience as a global trait; absence of distress; objective error rate.  
**Primary risks:** moralising error responses; impression management; confounding psychological safety of context with personal disposition.

### CTAP — Challenge–Threat Appraisal & Pressure Regulation
**Item IDs:** 9, 10R, 11, 12R, 19  
**Intended construct:** self-reported appraisal/regulation habits under pressure, including prioritisation and maintaining action under evaluative demand.  
**External construct families to review:** challenge/threat appraisal; coping appraisal; emotion regulation under pressure.  
**Explicit exclusions:** clinical anxiety, stress disorder, neurological stress response, physiological resilience.  
**Primary risks:** clinical drift; context dependence; self-report may poorly estimate actual performance degradation.

### FRUP — Feedback Receptivity & Psychological Flexibility
**Item IDs:** 13, 14R, 15, 16R, 20  
**Intended construct:** ability to receive credible criticism, separate performance feedback from identity threat, and update behaviour when warranted.  
**External construct families to review:** feedback orientation; psychological flexibility; defensive reasoning.  
**Explicit exclusions:** agreeableness, compliance, low self-esteem, therapy outcomes.  
**Primary risks:** treating all feedback as valid; rewarding deference; overlap with coachability.

**CMI discovery question:** do four correlated developmental factors explain item responses better than a broad single “adaptive self-management” factor, and are the four scores distinguishable enough to justify separate reporting?

## 3.2 Cognitive Learning Profiler™ (CLP)

Items: 20  
Dimensions: 4 × 5 items

### RKEX — Rapid Knowledge Extraction
**Item IDs:** 1, 2R, 3, 4R, 17  
**Intended construct:** self-reported ability to identify decision-relevant signal in dense or ambiguous information.  
**External construct families:** information filtering; comprehension monitoring; executive information processing.  
**Exclusions:** intelligence, reading speed, working-memory capacity, actual analytical accuracy.  
**Risks:** strong self-enhancement bias; confounding familiarity/subject expertise with extraction skill.

### TRSY — Transfer Agility & Synthesis
**Item IDs:** 5, 6R, 7, 8R, 18  
**Intended construct:** tendency to abstract principles and transfer them across domains.  
**External construct families:** analogical transfer; far transfer; relational reasoning; learning transfer.  
**Exclusions:** IQ, creativity as a global trait.  
**Risks:** far-transfer claims are often overgeneralized; self-report may not correspond to demonstrated transfer.

### ADUN — Model Updating & Adaptive Unlearning
**Item IDs:** 9, 10R, 11, 12R, 19  
**Intended construct:** willingness to revise obsolete rules and beliefs when evidence changes.  
**External construct families:** belief updating; cognitive flexibility; organizational unlearning.  
**Exclusions:** impulsiveness, novelty seeking, disregard for accumulated expertise.  
**Risks:** “unlearning” has multiple definitions; rapid change is not always adaptive.

### DGAD — Digital Adaptation
**Item IDs:** 13, 14R, 15, 16R, 20  
**Intended construct:** proactive, hypothesis-driven exploration of unfamiliar digital tools and integration into work.  
**External construct families:** technology self-efficacy; digital adaptability; exploratory learning.  
**Exclusions:** technical skill, AI literacy, actual software proficiency.  
**Risks:** access/opportunity confounds; age and role exposure; rapid technology obsolescence.

**CLP discovery question:** can self-reported learning-process dimensions be distinguished from general confidence and actual cognitive performance, and should behavioural tasks be added for stronger future evidence?

## 3.3 Commercial Instinct Index™ (CII)

Items: 24  
Dimensions: 6 × 4 items

### IUIN — Integrity Under Incentive
Items 1–4.  
Intent: maintaining truthful, ethically bounded selling behaviour when incentives favour exaggeration.  
Construct families: honesty–humility; ethical decision making; moral disengagement under incentive.  
Exclusions: legal compliance knowledge; global morality.  
Risks: extreme social desirability; self-report likely ceiling effects.

### REJR — Rejection Resilience
Items 5–8.  
Intent: maintaining constructive action after sales rejection without excessive carryover.  
Construct families: resilience/recovery; sales persistence; affect regulation.  
Exclusions: absence of emotion; generalized hardiness.  
Risks: valorising persistence when disengagement is rational.

### SOIN — Social Initiation
Items 9–12.  
Intent: comfort initiating purposeful contact with unfamiliar prospects.  
Construct families: extraversion/assertiveness; proactive behaviour; social approach.  
Exclusions: sociability as a global trait; networking quality.  
Risks: cultural norms; role/channel differences; introversion stereotyping.

### NEGC — Negotiation Composure
Items 13–16.  
Intent: maintaining deliberate communication and judgment during tense negotiation.  
Construct families: emotion regulation; negotiation behaviour; interpersonal self-control.  
Exclusions: negotiation skill/outcome, dominance.  
Risks: composure may not equal effectiveness.

### PIPD — Pipeline Discipline
Items 17–20.  
Intent: structured follow-through, opportunity tracking and next-step discipline.  
Construct families: conscientiousness; implementation intentions; sales process behaviour.  
Exclusions: CRM proficiency; objective sales productivity.  
Risks: organizational process quality may drive responses.

### SOLC — Solution Creativity
Items 21–24.  
Intent: adapting value propositions and solution framing to buyer needs rather than rigid scripting.  
Construct families: adaptive selling; creativity; perspective taking.  
Exclusions: product expertise; actual solution quality.  
Risks: overlap with sales experience; self-rated creativity inflation.

**CII discovery question:** does a six-factor behavioural architecture provide incremental interpretive value beyond broad personality tendencies, and which dimensions require external behavioural or performance-linked evidence before any stronger claim?

## 3.4 Executive Leadership Index™ (ELI)

Items: 200  
Dimensions: 10 × 20 items

- **SPRI — Strategic Prioritization:** items 1–20.
- **EXAC — Execution Accountability:** items 21–40.
- **PDCO — People Development & Coaching:** items 41–60.
- **TPSC — Team Psychological Safety Creation:** items 61–80.
- **CFNA — Conflict & Feedback Navigation:** items 81–100.
- **VABI — Vision Articulation & Buy-In:** items 101–120.
- **INSP — Innovation Sponsorship:** items 121–140.
- **SNBU — Stakeholder Network Building:** items 141–160.
- **ORUI — Organizational Representation & Upward Influence:** items 161–180.
- **CDQL — Composure & Decision Quality Under Load:** items 181–200.

**Intended interpretation:** broad self-reported leadership-behaviour profile for structured development.

**External construct families to map:** task/relationship/change leadership behaviour; coaching/developmental leadership; psychological safety; conflict management; vision/transformational leadership; innovation-supportive leadership; social capital/network brokerage; upward influence; decision making under stress.

**Explicit exclusions:** leadership effectiveness as an objective outcome; promotability; executive potential; performance prediction; “high-potential” classification.

**Major scientific risks:**
- 200-item respondent burden and careless responding;
- ten correlated domains may contain redundancy or higher-order factors;
- same-method self-report can inflate relationships;
- several constructs are inherently relational and should eventually use multi-source evidence;
- “psychological safety creation” should not be inferred solely from the manager’s self-view.

**ELI gate before pilot:** item-redundancy/content analysis and response-process study must precede recruitment. Pilot design must justify full-form administration versus planned matrix sampling.

## 3.5 Managerial Effectiveness Index™ (MEI)

Items: 50  
Dimensions: same ten domains as ELI, 5 selected items per domain:
- SPRI: 2, 3R, 10, 12, 15R
- EXAC: 22, 23R, 26, 28, 34
- PDCO: 42, 44, 48, 50, 60
- TPSC: 62, 64, 68, 74, 80
- CFNA: 82, 84, 86, 90, 98
- VABI: 102, 104, 106, 110, 118
- INSP: 122, 124, 128, 130, 138
- SNBU: 142, 144, 146, 150, 156
- ORUI: 162, 164, 168, 172, 176
- CDQL: 182, 184, 186, 188, 198

**Intended interpretation:** shorter developmental profile using the same ten conceptual domains as ELI.

**Critical evidence question:** does the 50-item short form preserve enough content coverage and score precision to justify ten separate five-item scores, and how closely does it reproduce the information carried by the ELI without being treated as psychometrically interchangeable by assumption?

**Required additional study:** after both forms have stable structures, administer ELI and MEI in a planned linking/subsample design to quantify short-form/full-form correspondence. High correlation alone will not prove interchangeability.

## 3.6 Metacognitive Executive Assessment™ (MEA)

Items: 20  
Dimensions: 4 × 5

### MCAL — Metacognitive Calibration
Items 1, 2R, 3, 4R, 17.  
Intent: monitoring confidence, recognizing knowledge limits and checking predictions against outcomes.  
Construct families: metacognitive monitoring; confidence calibration; overconfidence.  
Exclusions: actual intelligence or decision accuracy.  
Risk: self-report calibration is paradoxical—people may be poorly calibrated about their own calibration. Behavioural calibration tasks should therefore be considered.

### CCLD — Cognitive Control Under Load
Items 5, 6R, 7, 8R, 18.  
Intent: self-reported attentional prioritisation and interference management under work pressure.  
Construct families: executive attention/cognitive control; attentional control under stress.  
Exclusions: neurological executive function, ADHD, working-memory capacity.  
Risk: neurological/clinical inference must be actively prevented.

### AUNV — Adaptive Uncertainty Navigation
Items 9, 10R, 11, 12R, 19.  
Intent: acting under incomplete information while updating beliefs as evidence changes.  
Construct families: decision making under uncertainty; probabilistic reasoning; ambiguity tolerance.  
Exclusions: risk appetite as a global trait.  
Risk: confidence and optimal action depend strongly on decision context.

### MREG — Metacognitive Regulation & Flexibility
Items 13, 14R, 15, 16R, 20.  
Intent: detecting strategy failure and deliberately changing approach.  
Construct families: metacognitive regulation; cognitive flexibility; error monitoring.  
Exclusions: neurological flexibility or clinical executive function.  
Risk: self-reported flexibility can diverge from demonstrated switching behaviour.

**MEA evidence requirement:** include behavioural/forecast calibration components in later validation rather than relying exclusively on self-report convergence.

## 3.7 Self-Limiting Belief Auditor™ (SLBA)

Items: 20  
Dimensions: 4 × 5

### FRAP — Fraudulence Appraisal
Items 1R, 2, 3R, 4, 17.  
Intent: discounting versus appropriately recognizing evidence of one’s own competence.  
Construct families: impostor phenomenon; self-efficacy attribution; competence discounting.  
Exclusions: diagnosis, “impostor syndrome,” low self-esteem.  
Risk: clinicalized popular terminology; demographic/context differences.

### EXAG — Executive Agency
Items 5R, 6, 7R, 8, 18.  
Intent: willingness to exercise legitimate role authority without unnecessary permission dependence.  
Construct families: personal agency; self-efficacy; proactive behaviour.  
Exclusions: rule-breaking, dominance, organizational power itself.  
Risk: organizational hierarchy/culture may constrain agency appropriately.

### TBAV — Threat-Based Avoidance
Items 9R, 10, 11R, 12, 19.  
Intent: recognizing avoidance/delay that is framed as preparation when action is warranted.  
Construct families: experiential avoidance; procrastination/avoidance; threat appraisal.  
Exclusions: anxiety disorder; risk tolerance; impulsivity.  
Risk: prudence and avoidance can be difficult to distinguish without context.

### RSOR — Resource Orientation
Items 13–16, 20.  
Intent: tendency to interpret peers’ success/opportunities as expandable/shared versus zero-sum threat.  
Construct families: scarcity mindset; social comparison; resource beliefs.  
Exclusions: actual resource scarcity, socioeconomic circumstance.  
Risk: must not individualize structural scarcity or inequality.

**SLBA evidence requirement:** terminology must remain non-clinical. Expert review should include a psychologist familiar with impostor-phenomenon research and a fairness/context reviewer.

## 3.8 Strategic Inversion Diagnostics™ (SID)

Items: 20  
Dimensions: 4 × 5

### FPRD — First-Principle Deconstruction
Items 1, 2R, 3, 4R, 17.  
Intent: decomposing problems into underlying constraints and testing inherited conventions.  
Construct families: problem representation; causal reasoning; systems/problem-solving approaches.  
Exclusions: intelligence, superior strategic performance.  
Risk: “first principles” is often practitioner language rather than a single standardized psychological construct.

### SOFC — Second-Order Forecasting
Items 5, 6R, 7, 8R, 18.  
Intent: considering downstream responses, side effects and feedback after an initial decision.  
Construct families: prospective thinking; systems thinking; consequence mapping.  
Exclusions: forecasting accuracy.  
Risk: self-reported foresight may not predict actual forecast calibration.

### ASAU — Assumption Auditing
Items 9, 10R, 11, 12R, 19.  
Intent: identifying and deliberately challenging hidden assumptions underlying plans.  
Construct families: actively open-minded thinking; disconfirmation seeking; critical thinking disposition.  
Exclusions: skepticism/cynicism, general intelligence.  
Risk: “questioning assumptions” can become performative without evidence of better decisions.

### CXNV — Complexity Navigation
Items 13, 14R, 15, 16R, 20.  
Intent: recognizing interdependence, feedback and cross-system effects when diagnosing organizational problems.  
Construct families: systems thinking; complexity cognition; causal-system reasoning.  
Exclusions: systems expertise, objective strategic capability.  
Risk: broad construct; needs careful content delimitation.

**SID evidence requirement:** future studies should pair self-report with structured reasoning scenarios before any claim about actual strategic reasoning quality.

# 4. Expert content-review protocol for P1

For every item reviewers rate 1–4 on:
- relevance to intended construct;
- clarity;
- construct specificity;
- developmental appropriateness;
- cultural/context fit;
- social-desirability pressure;
- redundancy;
- construct contamination;
- clinical/identity-labelling risk.

Record RETAIN / REVISE / RETIRE / COGNITIVE-TEST.

### Decision rule
No automatic cut-score will determine item retention. Quantitative content-validity summaries may be reported, but decisions must combine ratings, qualitative rationale, construct coverage and disagreement resolution.

# 5. Response-process / cognitive-interview protocol

Probe:
- what the participant believes the item is asking;
- situations/time frames used to answer;
- interpretation of key terms;
- distinction between adjacent response options;
- hidden assumptions about role, culture or resources;
- social-desirability pressure;
- reverse-key confusion;
- whether the item asks about behaviour, confidence, ability or outcome;
- whether wording sounds clinical, diagnostic or morally evaluative.

For MEA, CLP and SID, explicitly test whether participants interpret self-report items as claims about objective cognitive ability.  
For CII, test incentive/social-desirability distortion.  
For ELI/MEI, test whether “manager/team” wording fits matrixed and non-line-management roles.  
For SLBA, test stigma, clinical interpretation and context-sensitive prudence versus avoidance.

# 6. Pilot readiness gates

An instrument remains **NOT READY FOR RECRUITMENT** until all are complete:
- [ ] construct map method-reviewed;
- [ ] literature search and evidence table completed with traceable sources;
- [ ] expert panel completed;
- [ ] cognitive interviews completed and issue saturation assessed;
- [ ] changes resolved under semantic version control;
- [ ] frozen item/scoring/report version recorded;
- [ ] preregistration frozen;
- [ ] external comparator licensing checked;
- [ ] participant consent/privacy reviewed;
- [ ] analysis dataset schema tested;
- [ ] analysis scripts scaffolded on synthetic data;
- [ ] study owner signs recruitment release.

# 7. Evidence-domain effect

Creation of this pack justifies **CE / CTE / UE = IN PROGRESS** for these eight instruments.

It does **not** justify:
- CE / CTE / UE = supported;
- any reliability or structural evidence;
- fairness evidence;
- criterion/predictive evidence;
- norms;
- consequential-use approval.

# 8. Methodological anchors

Programme design is aligned conceptually with:
- AERA / APA / NCME, *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014): validity evidence is tied to proposed score interpretations and uses, including evidence from content, response processes, internal structure, relations to other variables and testing consequences.
- SIOP, *Principles for the Validation and Use of Personnel Selection Procedures* (2018): consequential employment inference requires evidence relevant to the intended inference and use; this P1 pack does not authorize selection use.
- Syntropix Validation Study Protocol v1 and Assessment Governance Framework v1.

These references govern methodology; they do not endorse or validate Syntropix instruments.
