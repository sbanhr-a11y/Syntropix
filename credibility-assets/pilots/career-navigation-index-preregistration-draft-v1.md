# Career Navigation Index — Discovery Preregistration Draft v1

Study ID: SX-ST-CNI-DISC-2026-01
Instrument version planned: 1.0.0, subject to completion of content/cognitive review
Status: NOT FROZEN

## Primary questions

1. Do the 12 items provide usable response distributions and coherent item relationships within the four intended dimensions?
2. Is the intended four-correlated-factor structure empirically plausible in the discovery sample relative to simpler alternatives?
3. Are dimension and overall scores sufficiently precise for developmental interpretation to justify an independent confirmation study?
4. Do external-variable relationships, where licensed measures are included, broadly align with the intended career-adaptability construct family without indicating construct redundancy?

## Primary model

Four correlated latent factors:
- concern;
- control;
- curiosity;
- confidence.

Three items per factor.

## Alternatives to compare

At minimum:
- one-factor model;
- four-factor correlated model.

Additional alternatives must be theoretically justified before model comparison or clearly labelled exploratory.

## Primary item flags

Flag—not automatically delete—items showing:
- severe floor/ceiling concentration;
- very weak intended-factor loading;
- strong unintended cross-construct evidence;
- reverse-key confusion in cognitive interviews;
- redundancy/local dependence;
- content contamination identified in expert review.

## Reliability

Estimate omega where the fitted model supports it. Alpha may be reported secondarily. Interpret with uncertainty and alongside content coverage.

## External evidence

Comparator set: TO BE FROZEN AFTER LICENSING REVIEW.

Before analysing external variables, preregister:
- expected direction for each CNI score/comparator relation;
- which relation is convergent vs discriminant;
- multiplicity approach;
- whether the hypothesis concerns total vs dimension-level scores.

## Exclusions

TO BE FROZEN before analysing study outcomes:
- incomplete responses;
- confirmed duplicates/bots;
- technical failures;
- predefined low-effort criteria.

## Confirmatory boundary

No CFA result from this discovery sample will be described as independent confirmation if the same sample informed item/model changes.

## Public claim impact

At most, a successful discovery study may support wording such as:
“Initial pilot evidence is available for [specific property] in an India/English adult discovery sample.”

It will not authorize standalone “validated,” normative, predictive, selection or global-equivalence claims.
