> Evidence status must describe what has actually been demonstrated. Do not use “validated”, “predictive”, “normed”, “scientifically proven”, “bias-free” or equivalent language without the corresponding evidence.

## 1. Purpose and intended use
State the decision or developmental question the instrument is designed to support. Define intended population, setting, administration conditions, qualified users and prohibited uses. State whether use is developmental only or whether any higher-stakes use has separately documented evidence.

## 2. Construct definition and theory
Define every construct and sub-domain operationally. Separate: (a) established literature; (b) Syntropix design hypotheses; (c) instrument-specific empirical findings. Include a construct-to-literature matrix with source, population, construct definition, relevance and limitation.

## 3. Content development
Record item-generation method, SME/expert review, content-validity procedure, cognitive interviews, reading-level/accessibility review, translation/adaptation method, item revisions and retirement rationale. Preserve an item lineage table.

## 4. Administration and participant experience
Document instructions, response scale, expected duration, device constraints, accessibility, accommodations, consent/privacy notices, interruption/restart handling, missing-response rules and test-security controls where applicable.

## 5. Scoring specification
Define raw scoring, reverse scoring, weighting, scale construction, transformations, missing-data handling, minimum valid response rule, rounding, confidence/uncertainty representation and report logic. Store the executable scoring key separately under access control and checksum the released version.

## 6. Development and validation samples
For every study: dates, recruitment, geography, language, role/level, industry, sample size, inclusion/exclusion, completion rate, missingness, demographics relevant to fairness, incentives and limitations. Do not imply representativeness unless sampling supports it.

## 7. Item and response evidence
Report distributions, missingness, ceiling/floor effects, item-total relationships where appropriate, response-process findings and item-retention decisions. Explain thresholds before presenting them.

## 8. Reliability and precision
Report coefficients appropriate to the scale/model (for example omega; alpha may be included for comparability), uncertainty intervals where feasible, test–retest evidence where relevant and conditional precision/SEM when supported. Reliability is not validity.

## 9. Internal structure
Document EFA/CFA or other model evidence only when sample/design support it. Report model specification, estimator, fit indices, factor loadings, cross-loadings, residual issues, alternative models, hold-out/independent confirmation and limitations. Avoid post-hoc certainty.

## 10. Relations with external variables
Document convergent, discriminant and criterion evidence with instruments/outcomes, timing, sample, effect sizes, uncertainty and multiplicity handling. Correlation does not establish causality or predictive utility.

## 11. Fairness, accessibility and comparability
Define groups examined and rationale. Where justified, evaluate measurement invariance and/or differential item functioning, language equivalence, accessibility and adverse-impact questions relevant to intended use. State untested groups explicitly. “No detected difference” is not proof of absence of bias.

## 12. Norms, benchmarks and cut scores
Publish only if adequate sampling and methodology support them. State reference population, collection period, weighting, sample size, uncertainty, refresh cadence and applicability. Developmental bands must not be presented as population norms unless they are norms.

## 13. Interpretation
For each score/domain: what it may indicate, what it does not establish, appropriate developmental questions, uncertainty and contextual factors. Prohibit deterministic identity labels and clinical/neurological inference unless the instrument is legitimately designed and validated for such use.

## 14. Decision-use boundary
For selection, promotion, performance, redundancy or other consequential employment use, require a separate job-related validation/fairness/legal review and human decision process. A developmental instrument must not silently become a selection test.

## 15. Reporting and AI-assisted features
Document report-generation rules and any AI-supported reflection. AI output must be bounded as developmental assistance, traceable to allowed inputs, reviewable, and not represented as an autonomous employment or clinical decision.

## 16. Data governance
Document data categories, purpose, lawful/contractual basis as applicable, role access, retention, deletion, export, subprocessors, cross-border handling, incident path and versioned consent/privacy notices.

## 17. Limitations and open evidence gaps
Maintain a plain-language list of unresolved questions, underpowered analyses, population gaps, self-report limitations, common-method issues, language gaps and intended future studies.

## 18. Change control
Maintain semantic version, item-bank/scoring checksum, release date, approver, change class and evidence-impact assessment. Major construct/scoring changes require a new evidence review; wording-only changes still require traceability.
