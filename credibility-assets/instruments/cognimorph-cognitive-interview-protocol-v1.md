# Cognimorph — Plain-language candidate comprehension study (review-ready protocol)

**Version:** CMI-COGINT-2026-10-10 · **Status:** proposed / not launched · **Data:** collect only in a separately approved, access-controlled study workspace; do not put participant data in public GitHub or the POC.

## Purpose
Determine whether proposed rewrites of items 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18 and 19 are understood quickly and consistently by adults with varied English fluency, education and job background. This is *content evidence only*, not proof that the revised scale is psychometrically equivalent to the canonical assessment.

## Participant diversity and consent
Aim for 12–20 consented cognitive-interview participants in an initial exploratory round, recruited purposively across educational levels, English reading confidence, professional and non-managerial roles, and region/language backgrounds. This range is pragmatic, not a validated statistical power target. Avoid collecting unnecessary PII. Make clear the task is to evaluate the questions, not the participant. Participation is voluntary; no employment decisions, incentives or ratings depend on answers. Separate identifiable scheduling records from de-identified notes; define a lawful retention period and withdrawal/delete workflow under applicable privacy law.

## Procedure (25–35 minutes, estimated)
1. Read consent and brief introductory instructions. Invite participant to stop at any time.
2. Present each proposed statement alone, at normal screen size and reading order. Ask for a six-point answer *without explaining the item*; allow 'I don't understand' and 'not applicable' during the study.
3. Ask participant to say what the question means in their own words. Capture misunderstandings, unclear words and any interpretation different from the intended construct.
4. Ask for one specific real-life situation the person considered, without requesting private or sensitive details.
5. Ask how the person chose their response and whether a different situation would change it.
6. Compare to the canonical original only **after** the initial response; note whether the rewritten item may measure a different behavior or belief.
7. Reassure participants that confusion means the question needs improvement, not that they answered incorrectly.

## Exact probes
- 'What is this sentence asking about, in your own words?'
- 'Was there a word or part you had to read twice?'
- 'What situation did you think of when choosing your answer?'
- 'Can someone agree with part of the sentence but disagree with another part?'
- 'Would this question make sense to someone who does not manage a team?'
- 'Would your answer change depending on how safe it is to speak up at work?'

## Item-by-item hypotheses to examine
| Item | Main construct threat | Specific comprehension probe |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Deliberate rule use vs choosing priorities | Does 'decide what needs my attention first' mean planning or just reacting to what is urgent? |
| 13 | Stable self-worth vs immediate feedback reaction | Does 'feeling like a failure' mean mood, identity, or embarrassment? Is 'listen' a separate behavior? |
| 14 | Defensive reaction vs legitimate explanation | Does 'defend my plan' sound automatically negative or sometimes appropriate? |
| 16 | Judging usefulness vs disliking delivery | Can participants recognize advice as useful even when delivered badly? |
| 17 | Learning behavior vs belief in effort | Does 'hard work' mean time spent, willingness to improve, or career opportunity? |
| 18 | Learning after failure vs blame avoidance | Does 'without blaming people' exclude necessary responsibility and accountability? |
| 19 | Pressure appraisal vs job evaluation context | Do participants interpret 'judged under pressure' as stressful feedback, performance review, or a deadline? |

## Coding and decision framework
For each item and participant, code: initial comprehension (clear / partly clear / unclear), interpretation aligned with intended construct (yes / partly / no), confusing words (verbatim), context/access bias, double-barrel interpretation, response confidence, and proposed improvement. Maintain a de-identified evidence ledger and independent reviewer notes. Flag items for rewrite when multiple participants misunderstand the same phrase or when the wording introduces a different construct. Do not set a formal pass cutoff without an approved research protocol. The expert psychometrician and content reviewers must approve wording, intended construct, polarity/reverse key and version impact before any main registry change.

## After the interviews
Freeze item candidates, give each revised version a unique identifier/hash, then conduct a pilot with appropriate sample planning. Review response distributions, internal consistency, factor structure, subgroup fairness and comparability with the original version as sample size permits. Do not reuse existing norm/percentile/cut-score claims without validation. Keep the live Reviewer Programme and current canonical questionnaire unchanged.
