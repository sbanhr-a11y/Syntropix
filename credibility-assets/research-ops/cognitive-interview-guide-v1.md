# Cognitive Interview Guide — Assessment Items v1

Status: RESEARCH TEMPLATE  
Purpose: evaluate how participants understand and answer assessment items; not a psychometric validation study by itself.

## Sampling
Recruit participants reasonably representative of the intended population and relevant language/context groups. For age- or life-stage-positioned instruments, recruit within the intended age range rather than using convenience samples outside it.

## Opening
Explain that:
- the instrument is being tested, not the participant;
- there are no right answers;
- the goal is to find confusing, uncomfortable or misleading wording;
- feedback will not affect employment or service access.

## Per-item probes
Use selectively to avoid fatigue:
1. In your own words, what is this item asking?
2. What time period or situations did you think about?
3. What does [key term] mean to you?
4. How did you choose between adjacent response options?
5. Was any part ambiguous, double-barrelled or hard to answer?
6. Could two reasonable people interpret this differently?
7. Did the item make an assumption that does not fit your situation?
8. Did you feel pushed toward a socially desirable answer?
9. Did anything sound clinical, diagnostic, moralising or identity-labelling?
10. Would the meaning change in your culture/work/life context?

## Reverse-item probes
Check whether reverse-keyed wording:
- is understood as intended;
- creates negation confusion;
- changes construct meaning rather than direction only;
- produces accidental acquiescence/error.

## Scale probes
- Are the response labels distinguishable?
- Is the neutral midpoint meaningful?
- Did you want an option that was missing?
- Did you answer frequency, agreement or ability despite the stated scale?

## Closing
Ask:
- Which item was hardest to answer?
- Which item felt most repetitive?
- Which item felt most intrusive or judgmental?
- What important aspect of the construct is missing?
- Were any two items basically asking the same thing?

## Coding
For each issue record:
- item ID/version;
- participant/context;
- issue type;
- severity;
- evidence excerpt/summary;
- proposed revision;
- construct-risk assessment;
- decision and approver.

A wording revision that may change interpretation must follow the Assessment Version & Change-Control Standard.
