# Scientific Claims Redline Register — v1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: CONTROLLED / PRE-PRODUCTION
Scope: public assessment copy and credibility assets in clean PR #56.

## Governing rule

External research may support a construct, research tradition or intervention principle without establishing reliability, validity, norms, fairness, predictive utility or causal effects for a Syntropix instrument. Strong claims require evidence tied to the exact Syntropix version, population and intended use.

## Redline decisions completed

| Claim category | Previous risk | Current rule/status |
|---|---|---|
| Inherited predictive validity | Source-framework or literature evidence described as if it validated a Syntropix score | Rewritten: literature may inform construct design; Syntropix score is not established as a validated predictor unless version-specific evidence exists |
| Leadership outcome prediction | Vision/coaching dimensions described as predicting leadership outcomes | Rewritten to association-informed developmental language |
| Sales-performance prediction | Pipeline dimension described as a predictor of actual sales performance | Rewritten: research may examine process/outcome associations; Syntropix score is not a validated individual-performance predictor |
| Communication prediction | Vividness score described as predicting whether a message survives distraction | Rewritten as intended developmental reflection, not predictive measurement |
| Promotion/career determinism | Low feedback score stated to cause managers to stop coaching and user to be passed over | Rewritten as possible interpersonal/development risk; explicitly not a promotion/career predictor |
| Neurological/brain claims | Executive/metacognitive language implied direct brain or neuroscience measurement | Rewritten as behavioural/developmental self-report; no neurological measurement |
| “Elite” psychometric labels | High bands framed as elite cognitive/coachability/analytical capability | Rewritten as comparatively strong self-reported patterns within the instrument |
| Guaranteed intervention effects | Exercises said to prevent, permanently cement, destroy bias, or guarantee outcomes | Rewritten as practices intended to support/reduce risk/challenge habits; effects require observation |
| Strategic intelligence / forecasting | Strategic Inversion wording implied validated measurement of forecasting capacity | Rewritten as developmental self-report of systems-thinking, assumption and second-order mapping patterns |
| Source-framework transfer | HEXACO / communication research could be read as conferring source validity/reliability | Explicitly states that external evidence does not establish Syntropix psychometric properties |
| High-stakes use | Assessment copy implied readiness for selection/promotion/succession | Explicitly bounded away from standalone high-stakes use without job-related evidence, fairness review and human governance |
| Clinical / diagnostic use | Consumer instruments could be confused with attachment/personality/ADHD/autism diagnosis | Consumer runner and technical docs explicitly state non-clinical, non-diagnostic boundaries |

## Acceptable uses of higher-risk words

Words such as “predict,” “diagnostic,” “intelligence,” or “ability” are not automatically prohibited. They may remain where:
- they appear inside an item asking about the respondent’s own behaviour (for example, tracking a business prediction);
- “diagnostic” is part of the product name “Strategic Inversion Diagnostics” or means ordinary problem diagnosis rather than clinical diagnosis;
- the text explicitly says the Syntropix score is **not** established as predictive/clinical/normative;
- “ability” is ordinary-language description and not a claim of cognitive-ability testing.

## Evidence required before stronger claims can return

For a specific instrument/version, stronger psychometric or predictive wording requires a traceable evidence package appropriate to the claim, potentially including:
1. construct/content mapping and expert review;
2. item-development record and cognitive interviewing;
3. sample characteristics and administration conditions;
4. reliability/precision evidence;
5. internal-structure evidence;
6. convergent/discriminant or criterion evidence as appropriate;
7. cross-validation for predictive claims;
8. fairness/comparability analyses across relevant groups;
9. test–retest evidence where stability is claimed;
10. decision-utility/job-related validation for consequential employment use;
11. clear limitations and version/change-control history.

## Current publication posture

Professional Runtime v3, Suite v2 and Consumer Insight Suite instruments remain developmental/evidence-building unless an instrument-specific technical document cites stronger, version-specific Syntropix evidence. Marketing copy, report prose and procurement materials must remain consistent with that boundary.
