## E. Attribution assessment
Answer:
- What else changed during the period?
- Was there a comparison group or repeated baseline?
- Was assignment/exposure self-selected?
- Were measures known to participants/managers?
- Was follow-up complete and long enough?
- Is the observed change practically meaningful?
- What alternative explanations remain?

## F. Privacy and client approval
Confirm de-identification/aggregation, quote approval, logo/name permission, employee confidentiality, data minimisation and client sign-off. A buyer reference is not permission to publish a case study.

## G. Publication-ready case study structure
1. **Context** — organization/population and problem.
2. **Baseline** — what was observed before intervention.
3. **Intervention** — exactly what Syntropix delivered.
4. **Participation** — invited/completed and relevant attrition.
5. **Measurement** — sources, timing and method.
6. **Observed change** — values/effect with denominator and uncertainty where appropriate.
7. **What can be inferred** — proportionate to design.
8. **Limitations** — visible, not buried.
9. **Client voice** — approved quote, identified accurately.
10. **Next cycle** — what will be tested or improved.

## H. Claim ladder
**Level 0 — Illustrative:** product/workflow demonstration only; no client outcome.
**Level 1 — Deployment:** “X participants completed…”; process facts only.
**Level 2 — Descriptive movement:** measured pre/post change, with no causal claim.
**Level 3 — Comparative association:** comparison/longitudinal design supports a stronger association claim, still with confounds stated.
**Level 4 — Causal:** only where an appropriate experimental/quasi-experimental design and analysis genuinely support causal inference.

Marketing wording may never exceed the evidence level approved for the case.
