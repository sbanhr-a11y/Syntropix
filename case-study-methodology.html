CASE STUDY EVIDENCE METHOD / v1.0

No case study
before the evidence exists.

Syntropix will not publish a client outcome story simply because a programme was delivered or a testimonial was positive. This method defines the evidence gate that a deployment must pass before it can become a public case study.

Publication gateAttribution disciplineClient approval requiredLimitations stay visible

Publication gate

01 / CONTEXT

Who, where and why?

Population, problem, baseline condition and organizational constraints are documented.

02 / INTERVENTION

What actually happened?

Product, programme, duration, participation and implementation fidelity are stated.

03 / MEASUREMENT

How was movement observed?

Measures, timing, comparison basis, missingness and relevant operational data are documented.

04 / ATTRIBUTION

What can the design support?

Before/after association is not automatically described as causality. Wording follows the evaluation design.

05 / LIMITATIONS

What remains uncertain?

Selection, confounds, short follow-up, sample size and contextual boundaries remain visible.

06 / APPROVAL

Can it be published?

Client authorization, confidentiality review and any participant-level privacy considerations must be resolved.

Evidence-strength labels

LabelWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
Practitioner perspectiveNamed qualitative observation or testimonial.Effectiveness evidence.
Deployment evidenceParticipation, completion, action or operational-process evidence.Causal business impact.
Pre/post associationMeasured difference over time in the observed group.Proof Syntropix caused the change.
Comparative evidenceEvaluation includes a defensible comparison or counterfactual design.Universal effect across populations.
Current public case-study status: no Syntropix client outcome case is published until an engagement passes this gate. Controlled product walkthroughs remain clearly synthetic and are not substituted for client evidence.

Case evidence capture template

  • Client / sector / geography / population — disclose only with authorization.
  • Business or people problem and baseline.
  • Intervention architecture and implementation period.
  • Participation, missing-data and attrition details.
  • Primary and secondary measures, measurement timing and data provenance.
  • Comparison group, counterfactual or other attribution design if present.
  • Observed findings stated at the level supported by the design.
  • Limitations and alternative explanations.
  • Client approval, privacy review and publication date.
  • Evidence-strength label and any subsequent update/retraction.
