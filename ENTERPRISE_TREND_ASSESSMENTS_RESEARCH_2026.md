# Syntropix Enterprise Trend Assessments — Research & Commercial Blueprint

Date: 16 September 2026
Status: research-informed developmental instruments; Syntropix-specific validation is required before any claim that the instruments themselves are validated, predictive, diagnostic, or normed.

## Design principle

The two instruments below were selected because they answer current enterprise buying questions rather than adding another generic personality inventory. They combine organisational behaviour, consumer/market orientation, cognition, learning, human–technology interaction and behavioural decision research. Items must be original Syntropix wording; established scales inform construct definition and validation strategy but are not copied.

# 1. Human–AI Collaboration Readiness Index (HACRI)

## Corporate problem addressed

AI adoption is no longer only a technology-availability problem. Organisations need to understand whether employees can use AI with appropriate task framing, calibrated trust, verification, human oversight, learning transfer and team coordination. Research on human–AI teaming shows that adding AI does not automatically improve team performance; coordination, shared cognition, transparency and calibrated trust matter. Contemporary trust-calibration work also distinguishes both over-trust and under-trust as performance risks.

## Research anchors

- Schmutz et al. (2024), *Current Opinion in Psychology*, “AI-teaming: Redefining collaboration in the digital era,” DOI: 10.1016/j.copsyc.2024.101837. Highlights coordination, communication, trust and shared cognition as central human–AI teaming issues.
- “Calibrating workers’ trust in intelligent automated systems” (2024), *Patterns* 5(9):101045, DOI: 10.1016/j.patter.2024.101045. Supports calibrated rather than simply higher trust.
- C-XAI framework (2024), *Journal of Responsible Technology* 17:100076, DOI: 10.1016/j.jrt.2024.100076. Supports human-centred explanation and trust-calibration design.
- Research on decision control and explanations in human–AI collaboration reports that user control can improve perceived trust/understanding while explanations can also increase cognitive complexity; therefore the assessment should not equate “more explanation” with universally better performance.
- Experimental research on motivation and accuracy information in AI-supported decision-making supports deliberate evaluation of AI recommendations and balanced scepticism rather than automatic compliance.

## Six dimensions — 5 original items each / 30 total

1. **Task Framing & Delegation Judgement** — selecting suitable AI tasks, specifying constraints, separating assistance from accountable human decisions.
2. **Trust Calibration** — avoiding both reflexive rejection and uncritical acceptance; adjusting reliance to evidence, task risk and system capability.
3. **Verification & Evidence Discipline** — checking sources, assumptions, calculations, contradictions and uncertainty before acting.
4. **Human Judgement & Accountability** — preserving ownership, ethical judgement, escalation and consequential-decision oversight.
5. **AI Learning Agility** — experimenting deliberately, learning from failure, updating prompting/workflow practices and transferring learning.
6. **Human–AI Team Coordination** — making AI use visible enough for colleagues to coordinate, documenting material AI contributions and maintaining shared understanding.

## Response design

30 behaviourally anchored statements using a 5-point frequency scale: Rarely / Occasionally / Sometimes / Often / Consistently. Include scenario-style items and reverse-keyed items sparingly. Avoid asking whether a respondent “likes AI”; preference is not readiness.

## Report architecture

- Overall Human–AI Collaboration Readiness signature (descriptive, not pass/fail)
- Six-dimension profile
- Over-reliance and under-reliance watch-outs
- Verification discipline map
- High-consequence decision safeguards
- “Where AI helps / where human judgement must stay explicit” matrix
- 3 role-specific 14-day experiments
- Manager enablement actions
- Team operating norms
- AI Coach: reflection prompts, practice sequence, weekly check-in and remeasurement plan
- Downloadable individual report + cohort aggregation where privacy thresholds are met

## Validation plan

Cognitive interviews → pilot item analysis → omega/alpha → EFA → independent CFA/holdout → convergent/discriminant studies with technology readiness, trust-in-automation and relevant digital/learning constructs → criterion studies using observable verification and AI-workflow behaviour → test–retest → measurement invariance/DIF across major geographies, languages, job families and demographic groups. Until these steps are completed, label the product “research-informed; validation in progress.”

# 2. Customer-Centric Decision Intelligence Index (CCDII)

## Corporate problem addressed

Many organisations collect customer data but struggle to convert it into decisions across functions. The instrument is designed around the employee/leader capability chain between customer evidence and action: perspective taking, intelligence quality, behavioural interpretation, cross-functional sensemaking, experimentation and ethical value judgement. It deliberately differs from a sales-personality test and from a firm-level market-orientation survey.

## Research anchors

- Market-orientation research has repeatedly conceptualised the process around acquisition of market/customer information, organisation-wide sharing/interpretation and utilisation/response. Contemporary scale-development work continues to identify customer orientation, competitor orientation, inter-functional coordination and market intelligence as meaningful dimensions.
- A 2025 multilevel study of 557 hospitality employees and 121 leaders found employee customer-oriented perspective taking positively related to service quality, supporting perspective taking as a behavioural capability rather than simply a marketing attitude.
- Customer-orientation research distinguishes employee-level customer-oriented behaviour from firm-level market orientation and cautions against treating self-report as equivalent to customer perception.
- Workplace experimentation research (2024) shows that short, time-boxed experiments can support organisational learning and knowledge creation/retention, supporting an experimentation-to-learning dimension.
- Firm-engagement scale development (2024) identifies customer/employee participation, empathy, resource integration and management/cultural conditions as relevant engagement components.

## Six dimensions — 5 original items each / 30 total

1. **Customer Perspective Taking** — actively reconstructing customer goals, constraints, emotions and context without assuming one’s own preferences are universal.
2. **Signal Quality & Evidence Discipline** — distinguishing anecdotes from patterns; triangulating qualitative and quantitative evidence; recognising sampling and recency limitations.
3. **Behavioural Insight & Bias Awareness** — interpreting friction, defaults, incentives, context and choice architecture while checking internal confirmation bias.
4. **Cross-Functional Sensemaking** — sharing customer evidence across functions, resolving conflicting interpretations and connecting front-line signals to operating decisions.
5. **Experimentation & Learning Transfer** — converting hypotheses into bounded tests, defining success criteria, learning from disconfirming results and retaining lessons.
6. **Ethical Value Judgement** — balancing customer value, commercial value, transparency, long-term trust and avoidance of manipulative influence.

## Response design

30 behaviourally anchored items using a 5-point frequency scale. Include realistic micro-scenarios such as conflicting NPS/interview evidence, vocal-customer bias, dark-pattern pressure, feature requests versus observed behaviour, and cross-functional disagreement. Do not score “customer is always right” as customer centricity; disciplined customer centricity includes evidence, trade-offs and long-term value.

## Report architecture

- Customer Decision Intelligence signature
- Six-dimension profile
- Insight-to-action conversion map
- Evidence-quality and bias watch-outs
- Customer perspective blind spots
- Cross-functional friction hypotheses
- Ethical influence guardrails
- 14-day customer-learning experiment
- Manager/team operating moves
- AI Coach: customer-hypothesis prompts, evidence challenge, experiment design prompts and reflection sequence
- Downloadable individual report + privacy-safe cohort view

## Validation plan

Cognitive interviews across customer-facing and non-customer-facing roles → pilot → omega/alpha → EFA/CFA → convergent/discriminant evidence against customer orientation/market orientation, perspective taking, learning orientation and decision-quality constructs → criterion relations with customer/service outcomes only where role/context supports them → test–retest → cross-industry/geography/language invariance and DIF. Avoid universal predictive claims until criterion evidence exists.

# Enterprise commercial architecture — internal only, do not publish on public cards

Public enterprise competitors frequently use contract/proposal pricing rather than transparent per-assessment pricing. SHL states that product/report prices are visible to logged-in customers and Korn Ferry states that assessment pricing is contract-based. Therefore Syntropix should retain proposal-led pricing for enterprise use rather than publish a global list price.

Recommended launch hypothesis to validate commercially:

- India: ₹1,800–₹3,500 per participant for 50–199 seats; ₹1,200–₹2,400 at 200+ seats; minimum project fee ₹75,000 where cohort analytics/reporting is included.
- Rest of world: US$45–US$95 per participant for 50–199 seats; US$30–US$65 at 200+ seats; minimum project fee US$2,500 where cohort analytics/reporting is included.
- UAE/Saudi/Singapore: price from the global enterprise band and localise proposals rather than mechanically FX-convert India pricing.
- Facilitated debrief, executive interpretation, custom benchmarking, integrations, translations and remeasurement are separate scope items.

These are Syntropix launch-price hypotheses, not claims about competitor list prices. Re-price after the first 5–10 paid enterprise deployments using win/loss, willingness-to-pay, delivery cost and perceived-value data.

# Governance

Neither instrument is a clinical, neurological or medical diagnostic. Do not claim to measure brain activity, dopamine, neural plasticity or neurological states. Do not use either instrument as the sole basis for hiring, termination, promotion or other consequential employment decisions. Enterprise reports should retain human judgement, job relevance, privacy thresholds, fairness review, version control and explicit evidence-status language.
