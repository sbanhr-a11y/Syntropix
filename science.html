Evidence governance · living research register

Evidence before adjectives.

Syntropix separates evidence supporting a construct from evidence supporting a proprietary instrument. Published theory can justify what we investigate; it cannot, by itself, establish reliability, validity, fairness or predictive utility for a new Syntropix questionnaire.

Claims ladder

Level 1 — Research-informed

The construct and design draw on published literature. No instrument-specific psychometric claim is implied.

Level 2 — Evidence-informed developmental use

Items, scoring and interpretation have a documented rationale and may be used for development while evaluation continues.

Level 3 — Psychometrically evaluated

Used only when version-specific analyses support stated measurement properties. Sample, language, population and limitations must be disclosed.

Level 4 — Validated for a consequential use

Reserved for a defined use, population and decision context only when evidence supports that exact interpretation. Developmental evidence is not selection evidence.

Current guardrail. Unless an instrument-specific technical note says otherwise, Syntropix scores are developmental indicators—not diagnoses, clinical thresholds, neurological measurements, population percentiles or guaranteed predictors of performance.
Validation mechanism

What must be tested before claims become stronger

  • content validity: construct map, expert review and cognitive interviewing;
  • response quality, missingness, item distributions and item-total behaviour;
  • internal consistency using appropriate coefficients and uncertainty intervals;
  • structural validity using factor analysis appropriate to the item model;
  • convergent and discriminant evidence against defensible external measures;
  • test–retest evidence where temporal stability is part of the intended interpretation;
  • fairness, differential item functioning and cross-group/cross-language comparability;
  • criterion or incremental validity only when the intended use requires it;
  • versioning: evidence attaches to the exact item/scoring version evaluated.
Consumer Insight Series · version 1.0.1

Three new developmental maps for contemporary adult life

These instruments translate research-supported constructs into original Syntropix items and practical reports. They are deliberately designed for personal development rather than diagnosis, treatment, neurological inference or employment selection. Published evidence supports the constructs and design choices; it does not yet establish psychometric validity for the proprietary Syntropix instruments themselves.

Relationship & intimacy

Relationship & Intimacy Blueprint

What it maps: Closeness Security, Rejection Alarm, Distance Regulation and Repair Flexibility. The design is informed by dimensional adult-attachment research, especially the distinction between attachment anxiety and avoidance, while adding a developmental focus on communication and repair. It does not assign a fixed attachment identity or diagnose a relationship problem.

Current evidence level: research-informed developmental instrument. Instrument-specific reliability, factor structure, test–retest behaviour, convergent/discriminant evidence and cross-cultural fairness remain to be established with Syntropix participant data.

Shadow & self-regulation

Shadow Pattern Map

What it maps: Image Protection, Threat Projection, Impulse Leverage and Adaptive Ownership. “Shadow” is used as an accessible developmental metaphor; scoring is not a Dark Triad/Dark Tetrad diagnosis and does not label users narcissistic, psychopathic, Machiavellian, toxic or disordered. Contemporary dark-trait measurement research also shows why self-report alone requires caution and why construct validity cannot be assumed from a popular label.

Current evidence level: research-informed developmental instrument. The proprietary four-dimension model requires item analysis, structural evaluation, reliability estimates and external-validity studies before stronger measurement claims are made.

Attention & reward

Attention & Reward Rhythm Map

What it maps: Attention Capture, Activation Friction, Reward Sensitivity and Recovery Architecture. It describes everyday patterns in task initiation, attention allocation, reward responsiveness and recovery. It is not an ADHD, autism, executive-function or neurological screening instrument and does not infer dopamine levels or “dopamine windows.”

Why the boundary matters: research on social-media ADHD content has repeatedly found substantial misinformation and self-diagnostic risk. Syntropix therefore separates useful behavioural reflection from clinical screening and directs users with health concerns toward qualified professionals.

Current evidence level: research-informed developmental instrument. Clinical sensitivity/specificity are neither claimed nor an intended validation target.

Data-to-evidence rule. With appropriate notice and consent, versioned item-level response data can support the Syntropix validation programme. Analyses should use de-identified or appropriately pseudonymised research datasets wherever practicable and report sample characteristics, exclusions and uncertainty. Until adequate data exist, scores remain within-scale developmental descriptions—not norms or population percentiles.
Research watch · September 2026

Recent developments informing our current approach

2026 · cross-cultural validity

Content validation cannot be skipped when measures travel across cultures.

Haynes, Matsui & Kaholokula reviewed recent assessment practice and argue for translation/adaptation, expert review, qualitative interviews and psychometric evaluation rather than assuming evidence transfers automatically between populations.

2025 · measurement invariance

Cross-group comparison is an active methodological debate.

Kusano, Napier & Jost challenge mechanical use of measurement-invariance thresholds and argue that the intended inferential goal and construct-relevant cultural factors should be explicit. Syntropix therefore treats invariance as evidence to investigate—not a checkbox or permission to overgeneralize.

2025 · social-media self-diagnosis

Engaging self-reflection should not become accidental diagnosis.

Recent studies of popular ADHD content on TikTok report high rates of misleading screening or symptom content and substantial viewer self-identification. This evidence informs the non-clinical boundary of the Attention & Reward Rhythm Map and our decision not to convert relatable experiences into diagnostic labels.

2024–2026 · dark traits

“Dark” personality measurement needs more than a viral label.

Systematic reviews and observer-report research show meaningful measurement challenges across dark-trait instruments, including construct coverage and self-report bias. Shadow Pattern Map therefore uses original developmental dimensions rather than presenting itself as a clinical or Dark Triad test.

2025 · careers

Career adaptability remains a major developmental construct.

Recent integrative review work synthesizes career-adaptability research across concern, control, curiosity and confidence while highlighting the need to distinguish theory, measurement and contextual application.
