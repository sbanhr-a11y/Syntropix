Evidence governance · living research register

Evidence before adjectives.

Syntropix separates evidence supporting a construct from evidence supporting a proprietary instrument. Published theory can justify what we investigate; it cannot, by itself, establish reliability, validity, fairness or predictive utility for a new Syntropix questionnaire.

Claims ladder

Level 1 — Research-informed

The construct and design draw on published literature. No instrument-specific psychometric claim is implied.

Level 2 — Evidence-informed developmental use

Items, scoring and interpretation have a documented rationale and may be used for development while evaluation continues.

Level 3 — Psychometrically evaluated

Used only when version-specific analyses support stated measurement properties. Sample, language, population and limitations must be disclosed.

Level 4 — Validated for a consequential use

Reserved for a defined use, population and decision context only when evidence supports that exact interpretation. Developmental evidence is not selection evidence.

Current guardrail. Unless an instrument-specific technical note says otherwise, Syntropix scores are developmental indicators—not diagnoses, clinical thresholds, neurological measurements, population percentiles or guaranteed predictors of performance.
Validation mechanism

What must be tested before claims become stronger

  • content validity: construct map, expert review and cognitive interviewing;
  • response quality, missingness, item distributions and item-total behaviour;
  • internal consistency using appropriate coefficients and uncertainty intervals;
  • structural validity using factor analysis appropriate to the item model;
  • convergent and discriminant evidence against defensible external measures;
  • test–retest evidence where temporal stability is part of the intended interpretation;
  • fairness, differential item functioning and cross-group/cross-language comparability;
  • criterion or incremental validity only when the intended use requires it;
  • versioning: evidence attaches to the exact item/scoring version evaluated.
Research watch · September 2026

Recent developments we are incorporating

2026 · cross-cultural validity

Content validation cannot be skipped when measures travel across cultures.

Haynes, Matsui & Kaholokula reviewed recent assessment practice and argue for translation/adaptation, expert review, qualitative interviews and psychometric evaluation rather than assuming evidence transfers automatically between populations.

2025 · measurement invariance

Cross-group comparison is an active methodological debate.

Kusano, Napier & Jost challenge mechanical use of measurement-invariance thresholds and argue that the intended inferential goal and construct-relevant cultural factors should be explicit. Syntropix therefore treats invariance as evidence to investigate—not a checkbox or permission to overgeneralize.

2025 · careers

Career adaptability remains a major developmental construct.

Recent integrative review work synthesizes career-adaptability research across concern, control, curiosity and confidence while highlighting the need to distinguish theory, measurement and contextual application.
