Evidence before adjectives.

Syntropix separates evidence supporting a psychological or management construct from evidence supporting a proprietary Syntropix instrument. A theory being well researched does not automatically validate a new questionnaire.

Claims ladder

Level 1 — Research-informed. The construct or design draws on published literature. This does not establish reliability or validity of the Syntropix instrument.
Level 2 — Evidence-informed developmental use. Item design, scoring and interpretation have a documented rationale and are used for development while instrument evaluation continues.
Level 3 — Psychometrically evaluated. Used only when instrument-specific analyses support the stated measurement properties, with sample and limitations disclosed.
Level 4 — Validated for a specific consequential use. Reserved for claims such as prediction or selection only where the proprietary instrument has appropriate evidence for that exact population and purpose.

Current guardrail

Unless an instrument-specific technical note states otherwise, Syntropix reports should be treated as developmental indicators—not clinical thresholds, medical diagnoses, neurological measurements, population norms or guaranteed predictors of job performance.

What a technical note should disclose

  • construct definition and intended use;
  • item-development and scoring methodology;
  • sample characteristics and instrument version;
  • reliability and validity evidence actually obtained;
  • fairness/measurement limitations and known uncertainty;
  • date of analysis and conditions under which the evidence applies.

Our validation programme is progressive. Stronger wording should follow evidence, not precede it.
