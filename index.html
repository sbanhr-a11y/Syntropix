Scientific Annexure & Dimensional Architecture

Plain-language guide on how to interpret your subscales and apply them to daily work.

1. Mental Adaptability & Learning Curve (CPEF)

What it means in simple terms: Your belief in whether your brain can learn new, complex skills through practice, or if talent is fixed at birth.

Why it is important & Impact: If you believe intelligence can grow, you take on difficult projects without fear. If you believe it is fixed, you avoid challenges that might make you look inexperienced.

How to look at it: Treat every complex software, unfamiliar industry metric, or hard concept not as a wall, but as a muscle that strengthens with repetition.

2. Mistake Recovery & Problem Grit (ESLFT)

What it means in simple terms: How quickly your mind recovers when a project fails or a mistake is exposed publicly.

Why it is important & Impact: Mistakes are data points, not character judgments. High grit allows leaders to troubleshoot instantly without emotional paralysis.

How to look at it: Ask yourself: "What can I extract from this failure to make the next iteration bulletproof?"

3. Composure Under Pressure (PSAV)

What it means in simple terms: How you interpret the physical stress and adrenaline of tight deadlines and high stakes.

Why it is important & Impact: High pressure can either cause cognitive freezing (tunnel vision) or sharp executive focus.

How to look at it: Reframe a racing heart before a presentation not as panic, but as your body generating extra performance fuel.

4. Feedback & Peer Learning (DESG)

What it means in simple terms: Your ability to separate your personal self-worth from critical feedback.

Why it is important & Impact: Defensiveness hides blind spots. Welcoming critique accelerates professional growth exponentially.

How to look at it: Treat tough manager or peer feedback as free consulting data rather than a personal attack.
